# 04 – Hemodynamics, Shock, and Mechanical Circulatory Support

## Normal pressures (mmHg)
RA 0–8 (mean ~3) · RV 15–30 / 0–8 · PA 15–30 / 4–12 (mean 9–18) · PCWP 4–12 · LV 90–140 / 5–12
· Ao 90–140 / 60–90. Cardiac index 2.5–4.0 L/min/m². PVR <2 WU (normal ≈ 0.3–1.6 WU; ×80 = dyn·s/cm⁵). SVR 800–1200 dyn·s/cm⁵ (10–15 WU).

## Waveforms
- RA/LA: a (atrial contraction), c (tricuspid closure), x descent (atrial relaxation),
  v (venous filling), y descent (rapid ventricular filling).
  - Cannon a waves: AV dissociation; large a: TS, PS, RVH, pulmonary hypertension. Absent a: AF.
  - Large v waves: TR (RA) or MR/VSD/LV failure (PCWP). Note: v wave size depends on LA
    compliance; a large v wave is not specific for severe MR and its absence doesn't exclude it.
  - Blunted/absent y descent: tamponade (also ↓x? no, x preserved). Steep x and y ("M/W" sign):
    constriction. Rapid y descent: constriction, restriction, severe TR.
  - Kussmaul sign (RA rises with inspiration): constriction, RV infarct, severe TR, restriction.
- LV: "dip and plateau"/square root sign = constriction or restriction. Spike-and-dome
  aortic = HOCM. Brockenbrough-Braunwald-Morrow sign: post-PVC beat shows **↑LV–Ao gradient
  and ↓aortic pulse pressure** (HOCM); in fixed AS, pulse pressure ↑ post-PVC.
- Pulsus paradoxus (>10 mmHg inspiratory SBP fall): tamponade, severe asthma/COPD, massive
  PE, RV infarct; absent in tamponade with ASD, severe AI, LV dysfunction.
- Pulsus alternans: severe LV failure. Bisferiens: AI ± AS, HOCM. Parvus et tardus: AS.
- Wedge validation: mean PCWP ≤ PA diastolic (except large v waves), PCWP O2 sat >90%,
  a/v waves visible, catheter in West zone 3.

## Cardiac output
- **Fick**: CO = VO2 / (CaO2 − CvO2) = VO2 / [Hgb × 1.36 × 10 × (SaO2 − SvO2)]. Assumed VO2
  125 mL/min/m² (or 3 mL/kg/min; 110 in elderly) — assumed values are the largest error;
  measured VO2 preferred. Mixed venous sample from PA. Best in low-output states and TR.
- **Thermodilution**: inaccurate with significant TR (underestimates), intracardiac shunts,
  very low output, irregular rhythm; average 3 within 10%.
- Hemodynamic derivations: SVR = (MAP − RAP)/CO × 80; PVR = (mPAP − PCWP)/CO (WU);
  transpulmonary gradient = mPAP − PCWP (>12 abnormal); diastolic pulmonary gradient =
  PAd − PCWP (≥7 = precapillary component in CpcPH).
- Cardiac power output = MAP × CO / 451 (W); <0.6 W strongest predictor of mortality in
  cardiogenic shock (SHOCK registry).
- PAPi = (PA systolic − PA diastolic)/RAP; <0.9 predicts RV failure in AMI shock;
  <1.85 post-LVAD. RA/PCWP >0.63 also suggests RV failure.

## Shunts
- Oximetry run: SVC (high and low), IVC, RA (high, mid, low), RV (inflow, body, outflow), PA,
  and arterial. Significant step-up: ≥7% at atrial level, ≥5% at ventricular or great-artery
  level (single-sample criteria; averaged samples ≥5%/≥3%).
- Mixed venous O2 (with atrial shunt) = (3 × SVC + 1 × IVC)/4 (Flamm). Use SVC alone if no
  IVC sample; never use RA in the presence of an ASD.
- Qp/Qs = (SaO2 − MVO2) / (PvO2 − PaO2). Assume PvO2 = 98% if not sampled and no lung disease.
  Qp/Qs ≥1.5 significant (closure indicated with RV enlargement), ≥2 large; <1 = net R→L.
- Effective vs total flows: L→R shunt = Qp − Qeff; R→L = Qs − Qeff, where Qeff uses MVO2 and PvO2.
- Arterial sat <95% on room air without lung disease → suspect R→L shunt or Eisenmenger.
- Hemodynamic contraindication to shunt closure: PVR >2/3 SVR or PAP >2/3 systemic
  without reversibility (vasodilator test, balloon occlusion) — Eisenmenger physiology.

## Valve calculations
- **Gorlin**: Area = CO(mL/min) / [HR × flow period(s/beat) × 44.3 × √(mean gradient)].
  Aortic: systolic ejection period; mitral: diastolic filling period and constant 37.7
  (44.3 × 0.85). Hakki simplification: Area ≈ CO(L/min)/√(gradient).
- Gorlin underestimates area in low-flow states (flow-dependent). Use peak-to-peak for
  screening only; mean gradient from simultaneous LV–Ao tracings (two catheters or
  dual-lumen; femoral sheath pressure lags and overestimates pulse pressure). Never use
  pullback in AF or with variable rhythm.
- Severe AS: AVA <1.0 cm² (AVAi <0.6), mean gradient ≥40, Vmax ≥4 m/s. Low-flow low-gradient
  (EF <50%): dobutamine (5–20 µg/kg/min) → true severe if AVA stays <1.0 with gradient ≥40 or
  flow reserve absent (stroke volume ↑<20%) → still consider AVR (high risk); paradoxical
  LFLG with preserved EF → CT calcium score (≥2000 AU men, ≥1200 women = severe).
- Mitral stenosis: transseptal LA pressure is the gold standard; PCWP overestimates the
  gradient (delay + damping; shift the tracing 50–70 ms). Severe MVA ≤1.5 cm², mean gradient
  >5–10 at rest; exercise/pacing to unmask.
- Aortic regurgitation: wide pulse pressure, low aortic diastolic, rapid LVEDP rise
  equilibrating with aortic diastolic in acute severe AI (premature MV closure).
- Prosthetic valves: DVI, effective orifice area; patient–prosthesis mismatch indexed EOA
  ≤0.85 (moderate), ≤0.65 (severe).

## Constriction vs restriction vs tamponade
| Feature | Constriction | Restriction | Tamponade |
|---|---|---|---|
| RA waveform | Prominent x and y (M/W) | Prominent y, ↑a | Blunted/absent y |
| LVEDP − RVEDP | ≤5 mmHg (equalized) | >5 mmHg | Equalized diastolics (RA≈PCWP≈PAd) |
| RVSP | <50–55 | >55 | Variable |
| RVEDP/RVSP | >1/3 | <1/3 | — |
| Dip-and-plateau | Yes | Yes | No |
| Respiratory LV/RV systolic pressure | **Discordant** (ventricular interdependence; systolic area index >1.1, most specific) | Concordant | Pulsus paradoxus |
| Kussmaul | Yes | Possible | Usually absent (no); rises with tamponade only if effusive-constrictive |
| Other | Pericardial thickening on CT/MRI; BNP low | BNP high; conduction disease; biopsy | Echo effusion, chamber collapse |

Effusive-constrictive: RA stays high after pericardiocentesis.

## HOCM
- Gradient at rest ≥30 (provoked ≥50) is severe; provocation: Valsalva, PVC, isoproterenol,
  amyl nitrate, exercise. Increased by ↓preload, ↓afterload, ↑contractility. Alcohol septal
  ablation: see structural note. Mavacamten (myosin inhibitor) reduces need for SRT.

## Pulmonary hypertension in the lab
- Definition (2022 ESC/ERS): mPAP >20; precapillary PVR >2 WU with PCWP ≤15; post-capillary
  PCWP >15; combined if PVR >2 with PCWP >15. Exercise PH: mPAP/CO slope >3.
- Vasoreactivity (inhaled NO 10–20 ppm, epoprostenol, adenosine): positive = mPAP ↓ ≥10 to
  <40 with unchanged/↑CO → CCB responder (only idiopathic/heritable/drug-induced PAH).
- Balloon pulmonary angioplasty for inoperable CTEPH; reperfusion lung injury.

## Cardiogenic shock
- Definition: SBP <90 (or vasopressors) with CI <2.2 and PCWP >15, hypoperfusion (lactate >2,
  oliguria, altered mentation). SCAI stages A (at risk) B (beginning: hypotension without
  hypoperfusion) C (classic: needs drugs/device) D (deteriorating) E (extremis: arrest/ECMO).
- AMI-CS: early revascularization of the culprit (SHOCK: 6-month and long-term mortality
  benefit); **culprit-only** PCI in the acute setting (CULPRIT-SHOCK: 30-day death/RRT 45.9% vs
  55.4% with immediate multivessel). Norepinephrine first vasopressor; dobutamine inotrope;
  avoid pure vasodilators.
- RV infarct: hypotension, clear lungs, ↑JVP, Kussmaul; ST↑ V4R; avoid nitrates/diuretics;
  volume (cautiously), dobutamine, reperfuse RCA, AV synchrony/pacing, RV support.
- Mechanical complications: papillary muscle rupture (posteromedial, single RCA supply,
  day 2–7, acute MR often with soft murmur → surgery, IABP bridge), VSD (anterior MI → apical;
  inferior → basal; step-up at RV; surgery, delayed if stable; percutaneous closure possible),
  free wall rupture (tamponade/PEA; surgery), LV aneurysm vs pseudoaneurysm (narrow neck,
  posterior/inferior; pseudoaneurysm → surgery).

- Pressure-volume loop teaching: IABP shifts loop slightly left/down (↓afterload); Impella
  unloads volume and pressure (loop shifts left and narrows, ↓LVEDP); VA-ECMO shifts loop
  **right** and up (↑afterload, ↑LVEDP) unless vented.
- Hemodynamic support in high-risk PCI: BCIS-1 (elective IABP, no MACCE benefit; late mortality
  signal), PROTECT II (Impella 2.5 vs IABP: 30-d NS, 90-d per-protocol trend), **CHIP-BCIS3 (ACC 2026):
  Impella CP in LVEF ≤35% complex PCI gave no benefit and HIGHER CV death (26.7% vs 14.5%)**; PROTECT IV
  pending. Routine MCS for high-risk PCI is not supported. 2025 ACS guideline: microaxial pump in
  selected STEMI-CS is 2a; routine IABP or VA-ECMO in shock is Class 3.
- Post-arrest: TTM2 showed 33 °C no better than normothermia; 2025 AHA: temperature control
  32–37.5 °C for ≥36 h with fever avoidance.
- Escalation triggers on support: lactate not clearing at 6–12 h, CPO <0.6, PAPi <1,
  rising vasopressor dose; LV venting if PCWP >18–20 on ECMO, no aortic valve opening, or
  pulmonary edema.
