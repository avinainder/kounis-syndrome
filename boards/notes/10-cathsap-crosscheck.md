# 10 – CathSAP Key Points cross-check (ACC, April 2026 edition)

Cross-check of the 91-page ACC CathSAP "Key Points Study Guide" against notes 00–09. Of about
300 specific facts in CathSAP, roughly 225 were already in these notes. This file holds what was
missing, where the two sources disagree, and a short list of the points most likely to be tested.

CathSAP is written as guideline synopsis: few numbers, many "Class 1" statements. The exam is
written by the same community, so its phrasing is a good guide to how stems will be worded.

## A. In CathSAP, missing from the notes (learn these)

### Case selection and ACS
- SHOCK trial mix: ~80% of shock was LV failure; mechanical complications were acute MR 6.9%,
  VSR 3.9%, free-wall rupture/tamponade 1.4%. VSR mortality >80%; the others ~55–59%.
- Only 29% of AMI shock is present on arrival (NRMI); median onset 4.6 h after admission.
- Invasive shock definition: RA >10, PCWP >15, Fick CI <1.8 without or <2.2 with vasopressors,
  high SVR, cardiac power output <0.6 W.
- SCAD: DAPT in patients not stented may increase MACE and recurrence; hypertension is linked to
  recurrence, hyperlipidemia is not. Vessels heal within ~30 days.
- Takotsubo: 90% postmenopausal women; two-thirds have a stressor; LVOT obstruction ~20%,
  MR 14–25%, LV thrombus up to 10%; classified as injury, not infarction (4th UDMI).
- MINOCA work-up: three-vessel intravascular imaging for rupture/erosion, provocative testing for
  spasm, CFR/IMR for microvascular dysfunction; CMR abnormal in ~75%.
- Facilitated PCI (lytic or GPI before mandated PCI) has no advantage and more bleeding.
- Post-CABG AUC: PCI is "inappropriate" only in three asymptomatic scenarios (low-risk stress +
  normal LV + SVG target on no therapy; low-risk stress + normal LV + native target on therapy;
  intermediate stress + native target on no antianginal). Unstable post-CABG PCI is almost never
  inappropriate.
- Post-CABG PCI does not improve survival; it is for symptoms with ≥70% stenosis and ischemia.

### Procedural fundamentals
- Sheath French size = inner lumen; catheter French size = outer diameter.
- Hydrophilic sheaths reduce radial spasm; longer sheaths do not.
- Ventriculography complications include LAFB (plus arrhythmia, staining, perforation, embolism).
- Perioperative: hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3–4 days; holding ACEi/ARB the morning of surgery reduces
  hypotension; tranexamic acid for blood loss; ICDs reprogrammed intra-op and restored before
  discharge; low risk <1%, intermediate 1–5%, high >5% (MACE).
- Endomyocardial biopsy per CathSAP: 5–10 samples from more than one septal region; risk <1–6%;
  Dallas criteria have low yield in general HF.
- Guide support: passive (larger guide) vs active (deep intubation, contralateral root); wire
  crossing failures: hydrophilic/tapered wires, support catheters, blocking balloon, hairpin wire.
- VV-ECMO and IABP give no RV support; RV devices are Impella RP, TandemHeart RVAD, VA-ECMO.
  TandemHeart unloads by reducing LV preload (LA to arterial).
- VAMPIRE 3: distal protection helped only with IVUS-attenuated plaque ≥5 mm long.
- IVUS 20–40 MHz oversizes the vessel ~10%; OCT media-to-media is the default sizing when visible.
- Acute malapposition is common (15% IVUS, 50% OCT), usually resolves, no routine treatment.
- Rotational burr/artery <0.7 with DES gives outcomes like balloon-stenting; orbital crown must be
  advanced slowly (lumen gain ∝ rpm and inversely to advancement speed).
- Brachytherapy: beta sources preferred (limited penetration); cells in M and G2 are most
  radiosensitive; ≥5 mm margins; ≥12 months DAPT; no new stent at the time; repeat VBT at the
  same dose is safe; DES at a VBT failure site adds toxicity; Ir-192 gamma is compassionate only.
- CTO prevalence 18–26% of angiograms, up to 50% post-CABG; scores J-CTO, PROGRESS-CTO, CASTLE;
  expert success ~91%; collaterals do not relieve ischemia; symptoms are often dyspnea/fatigue.
- Bifurcation: crossover from provisional is easiest with TAP; high-pressure NC kissing for carina
  coverage when two stents are used; bifurcations carry higher stent-thrombosis risk.
- SVG: 17% of PCI is post-CABG (5% in SVGs, 0.3% arterial grafts); native targets 62%, SVG 32%;
  GPI do not reduce procedural MI in SVGs; atherectomy, laser and covered stents have no benefit;
  embolization risk highest in occluded SVGs, lowest in ISR and anastomotic lesions.
- Closure devices do not reduce complications, work best in the CFA, carry a learning curve;
  MANTA for large bore; device embolization and limb ischemia are rare but serious.

### Complications
- No-reflow risk factors: male, older, diabetes, dyslipidemia, CKD, large infarct, cardiomyopathy,
  long ischemia time. Cangrelor's benefit on stent thrombosis grows with lesion complexity.
- Perforation: epicardial collateral perforation may need embolization from both sides; reversal
  of anticoagulation waits until wires and balloons are out; post-CABG perforation can be
  catastrophic (loculated pericardium).
- Foreign-body retrieval succeeds in 70–90%; if it cannot be removed, deploy or park it somewhere
  safe.
- Access risk factors: female, age ≥65, low weight or BMI ≥40, hypertension, CKD, HF, PAD, AMI,
  high/low puncture, sheath size and dwell time, anticoagulation. Brachial access has femoral-level
  complication rates and limb-threatening ischemia.

### Noncoronary
- Right-to-left shunts are localized with pulmonary vein sampling; left-to-right with SVC, IVC, RA,
  RV, PA runs. Large v waves reflect atrial compliance and do not always mean MR.
- PH: BMPR2 is the commonest heritable gene; syncope or WHO FC IV means parenteral therapy;
  REVEAL 2.0 for risk; PVOD and capillary hemangiomatosis worsen with vasodilators; CTEPH treatment
  of choice is endarterectomy, BPA for selected patients.
- Tamponade: Beck's triad; surgery instead of pericardiocentesis for loculated, posterior, infected,
  recurrent, malignant or clotted effusions.
- ACHD: shunt closure when Qp:Qs ≥1.5 with PASP < half systemic and PVR < one-third SVR;
  perimembranous VSD closure risks heart block and AR; coarctation: stent for >20 mmHg (or >10 with
  AR, LV dysfunction or collaterals), or exercise SBP >220, or resistant hypertension; PS: balloon
  for ≥36 mmHg with symptoms, >64 asymptomatic; balloon-test for coronary compression before RVOT
  stenting; Fontan derangements are baffle obstruction, fenestration and venovenous fistulas
  (hypoxemia, stroke); any audible PDA is treated; TOF is the commonest cyanotic lesion in adults;
  arterial switch (<30 y) vs Mustard/Senning atrial switch; syndromes: Down (airway, PH), DiGeorge
  (hypocalcemia, immunity), Turner (aortopathy), heterotaxy (asplenia), Alagille (liver).
- Peripheral anatomy: artery of Adamkiewicz (anterior spinal cord) arises variably from the
  descending thoracic aorta; transaxillary access in the second segment; inferior epigastric marks
  the retroperitoneal border; IMA harvest can cause limb ischemia when it collateralizes the iliac;
  profunda supports the limb via geniculates; basilic vein is the straight path to the axillary vein;
  lumbar and azygos systems bypass an occluded IVC.
- Venous: IVC filter only for contraindication to anticoagulation or recurrence on therapy, never
  prophylactic; compression is the mainstay of chronic venous insufficiency; catheter therapy for
  DVT is established only for iliofemoral/May-Thurner.
- Renal: FMD affects the distal two-thirds of the renal artery and is treated with balloon alone;
  no revascularization for atrophic kidney <7 cm or chronic occlusion.
- Carotid: ECA supplies the brain via the ophthalmic artery in severe ICA disease; fetal PCom;
  ACA infarct = contralateral leg/shoulder; M1 = hemiplegia; M2 = face/arm > leg, aphasias.
- Stroke: tPA within 4.5 h even if thrombectomy is planned; thrombectomy for ICA/M1 within 6 h
  (6–24 h with imaging selection); basilar occlusion may be treated beyond 6 h; intracranial
  stenting only for recurrence despite DAPT.
- Structural: severe symptomatic AS carries ~80% 2-year mortality; BAV success = 50% gradient drop
  or AVA >1.0 cm², lasting 6–12 months; BAV contraindications include bioprosthesis, moderate+ AI,
  vegetation, dissection, bicuspid (relative); PMV uses Wilkins ≤8 or the Nunes score; only
  FDA-approved TMVR is SAPIEN valve-in-valve (valve-in-ring worse: residual MR, LVOTO); TR:
  diuretics are the mainstay, surgery for few; HCM: one-third no obstruction, one-third resting
  ≥30 mmHg, one-third provoked; SESAME is the electrosurgical septal-scoring alternative.

### Basic science, anatomy, pharmacology, imaging, misc
- Intima has three layers (endothelium, subendothelium, internal elastic lamina); coronary media is
  a single lamina of circular smooth muscle; vasa vasorum supply the wall.
- Sympathetic tone affects arterioles and veins; metabolic mediators mainly arterioles.
- Reperfusion therapy cuts infarct size ~40%; lethal reperfusion injury causes much of the rest; LV
  unloading could cut a further ~25%; microvasculature holds ~80% of coronary resistance;
  intramyocardial hemorrhage is the worst microvascular injury.
- Lipids: statin-benefit groups (ASCVD; diabetes 40–75 with LDL 70–189; LDL ≥190; 40–75 with
  ≥7.5% risk or ≥5% with enhancers); for <25% extra LDL lowering add ezetimibe or bempedoic acid,
  for >25% add PCSK9 inhibitor or inclisiran; PROVE-IT (atorvastatin 80 after ACS); IMPROVE-IT
  (ezetimibe after ACS); FH is autosomal dominant.
- Anatomy: commonest LCA variant is absent left main (separate LAD/LCx ostia); only a dominant LCx
  gives posteroinferior septals; venous system: AIV beside the LAD, middle cardiac vein beside the
  PDA, Thebesian valve at the CS os, valve of Vieussens at the great cardiac vein, CS diverticula
  with pre-excitation, vein/ligament of Marshall (left SVC remnant, AF source), commonest extra PV
  is a right middle PV.
- Pharmacology: peak P2Y12 inhibition is slower in ACS; limiting or omitting the post-PCI GPI
  infusion cuts bleeding without ischemic cost; 2025 ACS guideline gives bivalirudin Class 1 in
  STEMI and 2b in NSTE-ACS; CIN incidence 12–16% with eGFR <60 plus a risk factor, 20–50% with
  multiple; with DOACs in emergency PCI give parenteral anticoagulation regardless of last dose.
- Radiation physics: injury via Compton scatter and photoelectric absorption.
- Imaging: "AIR" (acquisition, interpretation, reaction); CCTA has high NPV and a CCTA-first
  strategy reduced death/MI (SCOT-HEART); T1 mapping/ECV tracks diffuse fibrosis in AS and HCM;
  exercise ECG alone is poor for restenosis; stress imaging is better.
- Statistics and quality: RCTs are internally valid but external validity is uncertain; QI
  analyses need time-series methods to separate secular trends; Donabedian triad (structure,
  process, outcome); ACGME accredits programs, ABIM certifies; A1c <7% for most adults; aspirin for
  primary prevention in diabetes only with >10% 10-year risk and low bleeding risk; CKD is not a
  strong restenosis predictor; age is among the strongest predictors of death after PCI.

## B. Where CathSAP and the notes disagree (which to use)

| Topic | CathSAP says | Notes say | Use on the exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warfarin before cath | INR <1.5–1.8 femoral, <2.0 radial | SCAI 2021: ≤1.8 femoral, ≤2.2 radial | Either way, radial tolerates a higher INR than femoral and vitamin K is not given. A stem with INR 2.0 and radial access is "proceed"; INR 2.5 is "wait" for both. |
| DOAC hold before elective PCI | 12–48 h by renal function | 24–48 h; dabigatran 48 h, longer with CrCl <50 | Pick the answer that scales with renal function and is longer for dabigatran. |
| Epinephrine for anaphylactoid reaction | "typically subcutaneous", IV only for the most severe | IM 0.3–0.5 mg of 1 mg/mL into the thigh | IM is current guidance; if IM is absent choose SC. Never 1 mg IV push. |
| Biopsy samples | 5–10 | 4–5 | Choose "multiple samples (≥4–5) from the RV septum"; reject any single-sample option. |
| Coronary DCB in the US | "do not yet exist" | AGENT DCB approved 2024 for ISR | The CathSAP line is stale. For ISR choose DES or DCB; brachytherapy for recurrent ISR. |
| Transcatheter AR device | none approved | JenaValve Trilogy approved 2026 | A stem written before 2026 expects "surgery is the treatment of choice for severe AR"; a stem naming a dedicated device wants JenaValve. |
| CMS and carotid stenting | 2023 proposal under evaluation | CMS NCD Oct 2023 covers standard risk | Notes are current. |
| Door-in-door-out | ≤45 min (2020 performance measure) | ≤30 min (guideline goal) | Both are correct in their context; the guideline target for a transfer decision is 30 min. |
| RCA dominance | 70–90% | ~85% | Same thing; left dominance ~8%, co-dominance ~7%. |

## C. Most likely to be tested (prioritized)

Ordered by blueprint weight and how often the point recurs across sources. Each is one line; the
numbers are the testable part.

1. STEMI timing: FMC-to-device ≤90 min (≤120 with transfer); lysis if PCI cannot meet that, then
   angiography in 2–24 h; rescue PCI for <50% ST resolution at 60–90 min, shock, HF or instability.
2. Stable multivessel STEMI: complete revascularization (index or staged) is Class 1; in shock,
   culprit only (CULPRIT-SHOCK). Occluded IRA >24 h in a stable patient: no PCI (OAT).
3. Routine aspiration thrombectomy in STEMI: Class 3; bailout only.
4. NSTE-ACS: no pretreatment with a potent P2Y12 inhibitor before anatomy is known; prasugrel only
   once anatomy is defined; timing by risk (immediate <2 h for instability, early <24 h for high
   risk, selective for low risk).
5. Radial access: Class 1 in ACS (reduces death, bleeding, vascular complications), 2a in CCD.
6. DAPT durations: ≥6 months CCD, ≥12 months ACS, 1–3 months in HBR (2a); stent choice never
   depends on planned DAPT length; DES over BMS everywhere.
7. AF plus PCI: DOAC plus clopidogrel is the default; aspirin for 1–4 weeks only with high
   ischemic risk; OAC alone after 12 months; no prasugrel or ticagrelor in triple therapy.
8. Cangrelor transitions: clopidogrel and prasugrel after the infusion stops; ticagrelor any time.
9. Bivalirudin: less bleeding than heparin plus GPI; more acute stent thrombosis unless a 2–4 h
   full-dose infusion follows (BRIGHT-4, VALIDATE); 2025: Class 1 STEMI, 2b NSTE-ACS; the agent
   for HIT (or argatroban). Fondaparinux is never the sole PCI anticoagulant (catheter thrombosis).
10. Enoxaparin: supplemental 0.3 mg/kg IV if the last SC dose was 8–12 h ago; full new
    anticoagulation if >12 h; never stack with UFH.
11. GPI: not upstream in STEMI; bailout for thrombus (2a); small molecules need renal dose
    reduction; benefit is mainly periprocedural MI; no benefit in SVGs.
12. Contrast nephropathy: eGFR ≤60 flags risk; prevention is isotonic saline and contrast volume
    (contrast/CrCl ratio); NAC and bicarbonate do not help; IOCM equals LOCM.
13. Anaphylactoid reaction: epinephrine first; steroids are adjuncts; premedication for a prior
    reaction.
14. Periprocedural MI (4th UDMI): troponin >5× URL plus ischemic evidence (ECG, angiographic,
    imaging, symptoms); >20% rise if the baseline is elevated.
15. Stent thrombosis mechanisms: early = underexpansion, dissection, geographic miss, DAPT
    interruption; very late = late malapposition, neoatherosclerosis, fracture. MSA is the strongest
    predictor of restenosis and failure; acute malapposition alone is not treated.
16. Intravascular imaging: Class 1 for complex PCI (2025 ACS, 2024 ESC); IVUS over OCT for the
    ostial left main; left-main MLA <6 mm² means ischemic; edge dissection needs a stent if it
    narrows the lumen, reaches the media with arc ≥60°, is ≥3 mm, or impinges the catheter.
17. Physiology: FFR ≤0.80, NHPR ≤0.89; IV adenosine 140 mcg/kg/min for ostial, serial and
    pullback; IC adenosine 200 left, 100 right; FFR is unreliable in the STEMI culprit (false
    negative), fine in non-culprits; CFR <2 abnormal but cannot separate epicardial from micro;
    IMR is the microvascular index.
18. Visual-functional mismatch: negative FFR with a severe-looking lesion means eccentric plaque,
    small territory, prior MI, collaterals, microvascular disease or artifact.
19. Calcium: burr/artery 0.5–0.6 (<0.7), 140–150k rpm, short runs, no decelerations >5000 rpm;
    orbital advanced slowly; IVL for deep or eccentric calcium; pacing for RCA/dominant LCx
    rotablation.
20. Bifurcation: provisional first; POT; wire the side branch when loss would matter; TAP for
    crossover; DK crush for true left main bifurcations with a diseased large side branch.
21. SVG PCI: embolic protection when feasible; no GPI benefit; no PCI of an occluded SVG in the
    first month (suture rupture); no-reflow treated with IC vasodilators (adenosine, nicardipine,
    verapamil, nitroprusside), not nitroglycerin.
22. Perforation: balloon occlusion first; covered stent via ping-pong guide for large vessels;
    fat or coils for distal/collateral; reverse heparin only after equipment is out;
    pericardiocentesis with autotransfusion for tamponade.
23. Access complications: retroperitoneal bleed = hypotension with flank/back pain after a high
    stick, CT and support; pseudoaneurysm = thrombin injection; AV fistula = low stick; 6 Ps =
    limb ischemia; radial occlusion prevention = heparin, patent hemostasis, small sheath.
24. Shock: norepinephrine first; dobutamine second; IABP has no mortality benefit (IABP-SHOCK II);
    Impella CP reduced mortality in STEMI shock (DanGer Shock); routine VA-ECMO did not
    (ECLS-SHOCK); ECMO raises LV afterload and needs venting.
25. Hemodynamic signatures: constriction = respiratory discordance of LV/RV systolic pressure,
    systolic area index >1.1; restriction = concordance; tamponade = equalized diastolics and
    pulsus paradoxus; VSR = RA-to-RV/PA oxygen step-up; acute MR = giant v waves; HOCM =
    Brockenbrough sign (post-PVC gradient up, pulse pressure down), peak-to-peak gradient grades
    it; AS vs HOCM by upstroke and post-PVC pulse pressure.
26. PH: mPAP >20, PCWP ≤15, PVR >2 WU; positive vasoreactivity = mPAP falls ≥10 to <40 with CO
    preserved; CTEPH goes to endarterectomy.
27. Shunt closure rules: Qp:Qs ≥1.5 with RV enlargement; do not close with PASP > half systemic or
    PVR > one-third SVR (PVR >2/3 SVR in the guideline wording) without reversibility; PFO closure
    for cryptogenic stroke in appropriately selected patients under 60.
28. Carotid: symptomatic ≥50% (CEA, or CAS when high surgical risk; CMS now covers standard
    risk), within 2 weeks; asymptomatic ≥60–70% with high-risk features and <3% perioperative
    risk; CAS gives more minor stroke, CEA more MI; age >70 favors CEA; EPD is required; statin
    before CAS.
29. Aortic stenosis: severe = Vmax ≥4, mean ≥40, AVA <1.0; low-flow low-gradient gets dobutamine
    (true severe if AVA stays ≤1.0 as gradient rises ≥40) or calcium score; TAVR is non-inferior
    or superior to SAVR across risk strata; pre-existing RBBB is the strongest pacemaker
    predictor; low coronary height and bulky leaflets predict obstruction; BAV only as a bridge.
30. Mitral: PMV for rheumatic MS with Wilkins ≤8, MVA ≤1.5 (or >1.5 with exercise PA rise), no LA
    thrombus and no more than mild MR; TEER for primary MR at high surgical risk and for
    secondary MR after GDMT (COAPT criteria); valve-in-ring is worse than valve-in-valve.
31. LAAO: non-inferior to warfarin; 90% of AF thrombi are in the appendage; post-implant DAPT
    for 6 months is now accepted in place of 45 days of warfarin; any peri-device leak matters.
32. Radiation: skin injury threshold ~2 Gy; air kerma 5 Gy triggers follow-up; peak skin dose
    >15 Gy is a sentinel event; operator 50 mSv/year (10 mSv × age lifetime), lens 150, skin 500;
    pregnancy 0.5 mSv/month and 5 mSv total on a waist badge under the apron; cine is 10–20×
    fluoroscopy; fluoroscopy time does not measure dose; table up, detector down, collimate,
    low frame rate, avoid steep angles.
33. Perioperative: no revascularization just to get through surgery (CARP); continue chronic
    beta-blockers, never start them the day of surgery (POISE); continue aspirin with stents;
    delay elective surgery ≥6 months after DES for CCD (3 months if time-sensitive), ≥12 months
    after ACS; perioperative MI is usually type 2.
34. Statistics: power = 1 − beta; alpha = type I error; NNT = 1/ARR; hazard ratios for time to
    event (Cox); non-inferiority needs both ITT and per-protocol, a pre-specified margin, and an
    underpowered trial proves nothing; PPV falls with prevalence.
35. Quality and regulation: NCDR CathPCI and TVT registries; AUC "rarely appropriate" PCI;
    Donabedian structure/process/outcome; device deaths reported to FDA and manufacturer within
    10 working days (SMDA); public reporting breeds risk aversion; informed consent includes
    alternatives.
