# 06 – Case Selection, ACS Pathways, and Lesion-Subset Technique

## STEMI
- Reperfusion goals: FMC-to-device ≤90 min (≤120 min if transfer); door-in-door-out ≤30 min;
  fibrinolysis within 30 min of arrival if PCI cannot be done ≤120 min from FMC (and symptoms
  <12 h, ideally <3 h). Age >75: half-dose tenecteplase (STREAM). After lysis: transfer all
  for angiography within 2–24 h (pharmacoinvasive: TRANSFER-AMI, CARESS-in-AMI); **rescue PCI**
  if failed lysis (<50% ST resolution at 60–90 min, persistent pain, instability).
  Lysis contraindications: prior ICH, structural cerebrovascular lesion, intracranial neoplasm,
  ischemic stroke within 3 months, suspected aortic dissection, active bleeding, significant
  head trauma within 3 months, intracranial/spinal surgery within 2 months, severe uncontrolled
  HTN, prior streptokinase (for SK).
- Late presenters: PCI for 12–24 h with ongoing ischemia, HF, shock, arrhythmia; asymptomatic
  stable occlusion 3–28 days: no benefit (OAT) → medical therapy.
- Multivessel disease in STEMI without shock: **complete revascularization** (COMPLETE:
  ↓CV death/MI 7.8% vs 10.5%; either index admission or staged within 45 days; PRAMI,
  CvLPRIT, DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI, MULTISTARS AMI immediate = staged, BIOVASC). Angiography-guided
  non-culprit PCI is at least as good as FFR-guided in STEMI (FLOWER-MI, FRAME-AMI favored
  FFR but small). In shock: culprit-only (CULPRIT-SHOCK).
- Thrombus: no routine aspiration (TASTE, TOTAL); bail-out aspiration for large burden.
  Deferred stenting not helpful (DANAMI-3-DEFER). Radial preferred. Bivalirudin vs heparin:
  equal with post-PCI infusion (MATRIX, BRIGHT-4 favored bivalirudin + high-dose infusion).
- Post-MI: DAPT 12 months, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker (REDUCE-AMI/REBOOT neutral if EF
  preserved; BETAMI-DANBLOCK favorable), ACEi/ARB, MRA if EF ≤40% with HF/DM, SGLT2i emerging, cardiac rehab.
  ICD at ≥40 days if EF ≤35% (≥90 days after revascularization).
- MINOCA (5–6%): CMR to find myocarditis/Takotsubo/true MI; OCT for plaque erosion/SCAD;
  vasospasm provocation.
- **SCAD**: young women, peripartum, FMD, emotional stress; angiographic types 1 (contrast
  staining/double lumen), 2 (long smooth narrowing, most common, 2A vs 2B to the terminus),
  3 (focal, mimics atherosclerosis; use OCT/IVUS). Conservative management preferred
  (spontaneous healing >90%); PCI only for ongoing ischemia/LM/shock (propagation risk; long
  stents, cutting balloon fenestration); avoid lysis; beta-blockers reduce recurrence; screen
  for FMD (brain-to-pelvis CTA); avoid pregnancy/OCPs.
- Takotsubo: apical ballooning, LVOT obstruction possible (avoid inotropes).

## NSTE-ACS
- Risk stratify: GRACE, TIMI, hs-troponin pathways. Timing of invasive strategy:
  **immediate (<2 h)** for very high risk (hemodynamic instability/shock, refractory chest
  pain, life-threatening arrhythmia, mechanical complication, acute HF from ischemia, recurrent
  dynamic ST changes); **early (<24 h)** for high risk (NSTEMI by troponin, dynamic ST/T changes,
  GRACE >140; TIMACS, VERDICT); **within 72 h** for intermediate risk (DM, CKD, EF <40%,
  early post-MI angina, prior PCI/CABG, GRACE 109–140); ischemia-guided for low risk.
- Complete revascularization is Class 1 in NSTE-ACS (2025). SENIOR-RITA (2024): invasive strategy in
  NSTEMI ≥75 y reduced non-fatal MI without a mortality difference. In cardiac arrest without STEMI,
  no immediate angiography benefit (COACT, TOMAHAWK).
- Antithrombotic: aspirin + P2Y12 (prasugrel after anatomy or ticagrelor); anticoagulant UFH,
  enoxaparin, bivalirudin (fondaparinux for conservative). No upstream GPI.

## Stable (chronic) coronary syndrome
- COURAGE, BARI 2D, ISCHEMIA (moderate–severe ischemia, no LM: invasive strategy did not ↓
  death/MI; ↓spontaneous MI but ↑periprocedural MI; better angina control), ISCHEMIA-CKD
  (no benefit, more dialysis), ORBITA (single-vessel: PCI ≈ sham for exercise time),
  ORBITA-2 (PCI vs sham without antianginals: PCI improves angina). REVIVED-BCIS2: PCI in
  ischemic cardiomyopathy EF ≤35% did not ↓death/HF hospitalization vs OMT. STICH(ES): CABG
  benefit at 10 y in ischemic cardiomyopathy.
- Revascularize for: symptoms despite OMT (class 1), LM ≥50%, proximal LAD ≥70% in MVD
  (survival benefit for CABG 2a), MVD with EF ≤35% (CABG 1), diabetes with MVD (CABG over PCI:
  FREEDOM, BARI 2D), large ischemic burden (debated after ISCHEMIA).
- Left main: SYNTAX score ≤22 → PCI reasonable (class 2a), 23–32 → 2a/2b depending on
  guideline and heart team, ≥33 → CABG. Trials: SYNTAX (LM subgroup equal), PRECOMBAT,
  EXCEL (5-y death/stroke/MI 22.0% vs 19.2%, non-significant; all-cause death 13% vs 9.9%
  controversy; periprocedural MI definition debate), NOBLE (5-y MACCE 28% vs 19%, CABG
  better), meta-analyses: no mortality difference, more spontaneous MI/revascularization
  with PCI. 3VD: SYNTAX (CABG better in intermediate/high), FAME 3 (CABG better even with
  FFR-guided PCI at 1 y; 3-y no difference).
- Use SYNTAX II/heart team; STS score (mortality, morbidity); EuroSCORE II.
- Ischemic testing before PCI: 2021 guidelines require documented ischemia or physiology for
  intermediate lesions (AUC "rarely appropriate" for PCI of asymptomatic single-vessel
  without ischemia).
- Diabetes + MVD: CABG (FREEDOM 5-y death/MI/stroke 18.7% vs 26.6%); PCI if low SYNTAX and
  patient preference.
- Prior CABG: PCI of the native vessel preferred over degenerated graft; redo CABG for
  diseased LIMA/LAD territory.

## Bifurcation lesions
- Medina classification (proximal main, distal main, side branch: 1 = ≥50% stenosis).
  True bifurcation = 1,1,1 / 1,0,1 / 0,1,1.
- Default: **provisional single-stent** strategy (NORDIC, BBC ONE, CACTUS/ BBK). Steps: wire
  both branches (jailed SB wire), predilate main (SB predilation only if severely stenotic
  or calcified — it risks dissection making re-wiring hard), stent main sized to distal
  reference, **POT** (proximal optimization: short NC balloon at the carina to the proximal
  reference size), re-wire SB through a distal cell, kissing balloon if SB compromised (TIMI
  <3, ostial >75%, FFR ≤0.80) or ostial SB dissection, re-POT. Sequential balloon (POT–side–POT)
  is an alternative to kissing.
- Upfront two-stent when: SB ≥2.5–2.75 mm with ostial disease >5–10 mm (DEFINITION criteria),
  difficult SB re-access risk, SB supplies large territory, complex distal LM (DKCRUSH-V: DK
  crush ↓TLF vs provisional 5.0% vs 10.7% at 1 y; DKCRUSH-II vs culotte; EBC MAIN: provisional
  non-inferior in LM). Techniques: DK crush (preferred in LM), culotte (wide angle <70°, similar
  diameters), T/TAP (angle ~90°), mini-crush, reverse crush (bail-out). Always finish with
  kissing and final POT; 2 stents usually need 7F guide (6F possible for DK crush/culotte with
  modern stents).
- SB occlusion predictors: plaque shift, ostial SB disease, bifurcation angle <70°, TIMI flow,
  main branch diameter stenosis (RESOLVE score). Rescue: re-wire, balloon, TAP.
- Imaging (OCT) reduces MACE in bifurcation PCI (OCTOBER).
- LM ostial stenting: extend 1–2 mm into the aorta, disengage guide, use marker/pigtail views.
  LM: MSA targets from note 01; IVUS strongly recommended.

## Chronic total occlusion
- Definition: TIMI 0 for ≥3 months. Indication: refractory angina despite OMT after treating
  non-CTO lesions is **Class 2b (ACC/AHA 2021)**; no mortality benefit (DECISION-CTO neutral, EuroCTO:
  QoL benefit; EXPLORE and REVASC: no LVEF benefit). Stop the procedure at air kerma >5 Gy
  (strongly consider at >7 Gy), contrast >3.7× eGFR, or >3 h; consider an investment procedure. Viability and ischemic burden support the
  decision.
- J-CTO score: blunt stump, calcification, bending >45°, length ≥20 mm, prior failed attempt
  (each 1 point; 0 easy, 1 intermediate, 2 difficult, ≥3 very difficult — predicts
  crossing within 30 min). PROGRESS-CTO: proximal cap ambiguity, moderate/severe tortuosity,
  absence of interventional collaterals, circumflex CTO (predicts success).
- Dual injection is mandatory for retrograde collateral assessment and distal target
  definition. Hybrid algorithm: antegrade wire escalation (polymer-jacketed tapered wires
  like Fielder XT, then Gaia, Confianza/Pilot family; penetration force 0.8–12 g), antegrade
  dissection re-entry (CrossBoss/Stingray; knuckle wire), retrograde via septal (safer, can be
  dilated with 1.5 balloon) or epicardial collaterals (never dilate, perforation → tamponade
  in non-post-CABG; post-CABG tamponade can be loculated), reverse CART (controlled antegrade
  and retrograde tracking — balloon on antegrade wire, retrograde wire enters), guide extension
  facilitated. Tip-in, rendezvous techniques. Externalization with RG3/R350 wires.
- Equipment: 8F guides (support), microcatheters (Corsair, Turnpike, Caravel; SuperCross
  angled), anchor balloon, trapping balloon for wire exchange, knuckle wire (Fielder XT/Gladius).
- Complications: perforation (esp. epicardial collaterals), donor vessel dissection/thrombosis
  (dangerous: donor vessel supplies both territories — anticoagulate ACT >350 s for retrograde),
  contrast nephropathy, radiation injury (long cases; air kerma), wire fracture, septal
  hematoma, equipment entrapment.
- Post-CTO: DES, DAPT 12 months; "subintimal" plaque modification (investment procedure) if
  failed: re-attempt in 6–8 weeks.

## Calcified lesions
- Decide by imaging: IVUS arc >270° or OCT calcium score ≥4 (arc >180°, thickness >0.5 mm,
  length >5 mm) → modify calcium before stenting. Angiographic clues: calcium both sides of the
  lumen without contrast; balloon "dog-bone"; non-crossable.

  fresh stents (burr entrapment) though "stentablation" is described for chronic ones.
- Calcium nodules on OCT: protruding, eruptive; highest ISR/ST risk; orbital/rota or IVL.

## Saphenous vein grafts
- Graft failure: early (<1 month) technical/thrombosis; 1 month–1 y intimal hyperplasia; >1 y
  atherosclerosis (diffuse, friable, thrombus). 10-y patency ~50% (vein) vs >90% (LIMA-LAD).
- PCI of SVG: embolic protection device (class 2a; SAFER: distal occlusion ↓MACE; FIRE:
  FilterWire equivalent; proximal occlusion Proxis); no-reflow prophylaxis (IC vasodilators,
  nicardipine); avoid predilation; direct stenting; undersized stents (0.9:1) reduce
  embolization; DES = BMS (DIVA: no difference; ISAR-CABG DES better at 1 y); covered stents no
  benefit (SYMBIOT, BARRICADE). Distal anastomotic lesions: balloon alone often OK.
- Prefer native-vessel PCI over degenerated SVG when feasible (class 2a); chronically
  occluded SVG: do not intervene; prior CABG protects the LM if grafts patent ("protected LM").
- Arterial graft PCI: LIMA spasm (vasodilators), tortuous; avoid oversizing; radial grafts
  spasm-prone.
- Guides: right JR4/multipurpose for RCA grafts (inferior takeoff), LCB/JR/AL1 for left grafts
  (superior/anterior takeoff), IM guide for LIMA (left radial access easiest).

## Thrombus and other subsets
- Aspiration bail-out; GPI; direct stenting; undersize and postdilate later. OCT for erosion
  (young, women, smokers) → antithrombotic without stent (EROSION study) in selected cases.
- Ostial lesions: aorto-ostial (RCA/LM) need precise placement, protrude 1 mm; ostial LAD/LCx
  — consider crossover to LM (bifurcation rules). Szabo technique.
- Small vessels (<2.75 mm): DES thin-strut (BIOSCIENCE), DCB non-inferior (BASKET-SMALL 2).
- Long lesions/diffuse disease: full-metal jacket vs CABG; imaging.
- Tortuosity: extra-support wires, guide extensions (GuideLiner/Telescope 5-in-6, 6-in-7),
  buddy wire, anchor balloon, deep-seated guide; sheathless guides.
- Aneurysms/ectasia: no stents unless covered; anticoagulation debated.
- Coronary artery bypass graft "string sign" of LIMA: competitive flow — treat the native
  stenosis, not the LIMA.
- Kounis syndrome: allergic ACS (mast cell mediators); type I vasospasm, II plaque rupture,
  III stent thrombosis; treat allergy and ACS in parallel (avoid morphine/beta-blockers
  cautiously).

## Guide catheters and wires (fast facts)
- Guide inner diameters: 5F 0.056–0.058", 6F ~0.070–0.071", 7F ~0.078–0.081", 8F ~0.088–0.090".
  Two stents side by side need ≥0.070" ×2 ≈ 7F (thin-strut 6F possible); 2.0 burr needs 8F.
- Left system: JL4 femoral (JL3.5 radial), EBU/XB 3.5–4 (extra back-up, ↑dissection risk),
  AL2 for very superior/anomalous; right: JR4, AL1 for shepherd's crook or SVG, AR mod,
  MP for inferior RCA/SVG, IM for LIMA; Tiger/TIG single catheter for radial diagnostic.
- Wire tip loads: workhorse 0.5–1 g (BMW, Runthrough, Sion); polymer-jacketed (Fielder FC/XT,
  Whisper, Pilot 50/150/200: 1.5–4 g — perforation risk); stiff tapered (Gaia 1st–3rd 1.7–4.5 g,
  Confianza Pro 9/12 g, Hornet 14 g, Miracle 3–12 g); support wires (Grand Slam, Iron Man,
  Mailman) for device delivery; Rotawire (0.009") for rota; ViperWire for orbital; 0.014" is
  the standard coronary wire; 0.035" for diagnostics; Wiggle wire for tortuosity.
- Balloons: compliant (grows with pressure), semi-compliant, non-compliant (NC: uniform,
  high pressure, post-dilation); nominal vs rated burst pressure (RBP; exceed at your own
  risk); stent nominal, post-dilate to ≥1:1 with NC.
- Stent platforms: cobalt-chrome/platinum-chrome thin struts (60–80 µm); strut thickness ↓ →
  ↓restenosis/ST; max post-dilation limits (e.g., 2.25–2.5 mm stents to ~3.5 mm; 3.0–4.0 to 5.5–5.75).
- Microcatheters vs OTW balloons: exchange with trapping (in 7F+) or extension wire (300 cm).
