# 02 – Pharmacology

## Procedural anticoagulation
| Drug | Dose in PCI | Monitoring / target | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unfractionated heparin | 70–100 U/kg (50–70 U/kg with GPI) | ACT 250–300 s HemoTec; 300–350 s Hemochron; 200–250 s with GPI | Binds AT-III; t½ ~60–90 min; reversal protamine 1 mg/100 U given in last 2–3 h (max 50 mg, slow). | Bivalirudin | 0.75 mg/kg bolus, 1.75 mg/kg/h; CrCl <30: 1.0 mg/kg/h; dialysis 0.25 mg/kg/h | ACT rises (not needed to titrate) | Direct thrombin inhibitor; t½ 25 min; no reversal; less bleeding vs UFH+GPI (ACUITY, HORIZONS-AMI) but ↑acute stent thrombosis in STEMI unless post-PCI infusion continued 2–4 h at PCI dose (BRIGHT, MATRIX). | Enoxaparin | 0.5 mg/kg IV (STEEPLE, ATOLL); if last SC dose <8 h nothing; 8–12 h add 0.3 mg/kg IV; >12 h full IV dose | Anti-Xa (not ACT) | Never "cross" with UFH (↑bleeding, SYNERGY). Renal accumulation |
| Fondaparinux | Not for PCI alone | — | Catheter thrombosis (OASIS-5/6) → give UFH during PCI |
| Argatroban | 350 µg/kg bolus, 25 µg/kg/min | ACT 300–450 | HIT alternative; hepatic clearance |

**HIT**: 4Ts score; stop all heparin (incl. flushes, coated catheters); bivalirudin/argatroban;
no platelet transfusions; no warfarin until platelets recovered.

## Oral P2Y12 inhibitors

| Load / maintenance | 600 mg / 75 mg | 60 mg / 10 mg (5 mg if <60 kg or ≥75 y) | 180 mg / 90 mg BID (60 mg BID after 1 y, PEGASUS) |
| Type | Thienopyridine prodrug, 2-step CYP2C19 activation; irreversible | Thienopyridine prodrug, 1-step (esterases + CYP3A4/2B6); irreversible | Cyclopentyl-triazolo-pyrimidine, direct-acting, reversible; active metabolite via CYP3A4 |

- ISAR-REACT 5: in ACS with planned invasive strategy, **prasugrel** (given after anatomy in
  NSTE-ACS) beat ticagrelor for death/MI/stroke (6.9% vs 9.3%) with no excess bleeding.
- Pretreatment: STEMI – load at first medical contact. NSTE-ACS – ESC 2020 says no routine
  pretreatment if going to early angiography; clopidogrel pretreatment reasonable if delay.
- Switching: escalation (clopidogrel → ticagrelor/prasugrel) load anytime; de-escalation from
  ticagrelor to clopidogrel: 600 mg clopidogrel load 24 h after last ticagrelor dose.
- Genotype-/platelet-function-guided: TAILOR-PCI negative; POPular Genetics (STEMI) safe
  de-escalation; TROPICAL-ACS; ALPHEUS.

## IV antiplatelets
- **Cangrelor**: IV ATP analog, reversible, t½ 3–6 min, full recovery 60 min. 30 µg/kg bolus
  then 4 µg/kg/min for ≥2 h or duration of PCI. CHAMPION PHOENIX: ↓periprocedural MI/ST vs
  clopidogrel. Transition: ticagrelor any time (during or after); clopidogrel/prasugrel
  **only after** infusion stops (cangrelor blocks their binding). Bridging for surgery
  (BRIDGE trial, 0.75 µg/kg/min).
- **GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors** (bail-out for thrombus/no-reflow, not routine):
  - Eptifibatide: 180 µg/kg bolus ×2 (10 min apart), 2 µg/kg/min; CrCl <50: 1 µg/kg/min;
    contraindicated on dialysis. Renal clearance; t½ 2.5 h; platelet recovery 4–8 h.
  - Tirofiban: high-dose bolus 25 µg/kg, 0.15 µg/kg/min (halve infusion if CrCl ≤60).
  - Abciximab (no longer marketed): Fab fragment, long platelet effect, thrombocytopenia
    (1–5%), reversible only with platelet transfusion; eptifibatide/tirofiban: wait 4 h.
  - Thrombocytopenia within hours of GPI: stop drug, check pseudothrombocytopenia (citrate
    tube), transfuse if bleeding.
  - Intracoronary vs IV: no clear benefit (AIDA STEMI, INFUSE-AMI ↓infarct size only).

## Aspirin
- 162–325 mg load (non-enteric, chewed); 81 mg maintenance (ADAPTABLE: 81 = 325). Irreversible
  COX-1 acetylation. NSAIDs (ibuprofen) block the site if given before aspirin. Aspirin allergy
  → rapid desensitization protocol; clopidogrel monotherapy if not feasible.
- **2025 ACC/AHA ACS: transition to ticagrelor monotherapy ≥1 month after PCI is Class 1** if DAPT is
  tolerated (TWILIGHT, TICO, ULTIMATE-DAPT, T-PASS). HBR: stop aspirin at 1–3 months, keep the P2Y12
  inhibitor (2a). De-escalation to clopidogrel after 1 month: 2b. STOPDAPT-2 ACS: early clopidogrel
  monotherapy was NOT non-inferior in ACS (more MI). HOST-EXAM / SMART-CHOICE 3: chronic clopidogrel
  beats aspirin monotherapy. Cangrelor: 2b. Routine GPI: Class 3, bailout 2a.

## DAPT duration – the decision map
- **ACS**: 12 months default (ticagrelor or prasugrel preferred). Extend beyond 12 months
  with ticagrelor 60 mg BID (PEGASUS) or DAPT-trial-style if low bleeding risk and high
  ischemic risk (DAPT score ≥2). Shorten to 3–6 months (or P2Y12 monotherapy after 1–3
  months) if HBR (ARC-HBR: ≥1 major or ≥2 minor criteria).
- **Chronic coronary syndrome**: 6 months default; 1–3 months if HBR (MASTER DAPT: 1 month
  in HBR non-inferior); 12+ months if high ischemic risk.
- **DAPT trial**: 30 vs 12 months: ↓ST and MI, ↑moderate/severe bleeding, ↑non-CV death
  signal. PRECISE-DAPT ≥25 → short DAPT.
- **Triple therapy (AF + PCI)**: WOEST, PIONEER AF-PCI, RE-DUAL PCI, AUGUSTUS, ENTRUST-AF PCI:
  DOAC + clopidogrel with aspirin stopped at discharge (up to 1 week; ≤30 days if very high
  ischemic risk). Clopidogrel is the P2Y12 of choice (avoid prasugrel/ticagrelor in triple).
  AUGUSTUS 2×2: apixaban < warfarin for bleeding; aspirin ↑bleeding without ischemic benefit.
  After 12 months, OAC alone (AFIRE: rivaroxaban monotherapy).
- **Bridging**: hold clopidogrel 5 d, prasugrel 7 d, ticagrelor 5 d before CABG; cangrelor
  bridge if recent stent.

## Contrast media and contrast nephropathy
- Types by osmolality: high-osmolar ionic (diatrizoate, >1500 mOsm; obsolete),
  low-osmolar (iohexol, iopamidol, ioversol; ~600–800), iso-osmolar (iodixanol, ~290).
  Iso- vs low-osmolar: no consistent CIN difference (NEPHRIC vs later trials).
- CI-AKI definition: creatinine ↑ ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h or ≥1.5× within 7 days.
  Peak day 3–5, recovers 1–3 weeks. Risk score: Mehran (hypotension, IABP, HF, age >75,
  anemia, DM, contrast volume, CKD).
- Prevention that works: **limit contrast volume** (keep volume/CrCl ratio <2–3; volume ≤3.7 ×
  eGFR), **isotonic IV hydration** (1–1.5 mL/kg/h 6–12 h pre and post; LVEDP-guided in
  POSEIDON), stop nephrotoxins, radial access, staged procedures, ultra-low contrast
  techniques (IVUS-guided). Does NOT work: N-acetylcysteine and sodium bicarbonate (PRESERVE),
  prophylactic dialysis, fenoldopam, statin loading (mixed; still reasonable), forced diuresis
  without matched hydration (RenalGuard has support). AMACING: no hydration non-inferior in
  low-risk eGFR >30.
- Metformin: no need to hold if eGFR ≥30 and no AKI; hold 48 h after contrast if eGFR <30 or
  AKI (lactic acidosis risk from accumulation, not nephrotoxicity).
- Contrast allergy prophylaxis: prednisone 50 mg PO at 13, 7, 1 h + diphenhydramine 50 mg
  1 h before; emergent: hydrocortisone 200 mg IV q4h until done + diphenhydramine. Shellfish
  allergy is NOT a risk factor; prior contrast reaction and asthma/atopy are.
- Thyroid: iodine load can precipitate thyrotoxicosis (Jod-Basedow) in autonomous nodules;
  hypothyroidism (Wolff-Chaikoff) in Hashimoto's. Delays radioiodine therapy 6–8 weeks.
- Gadolinium and CO2 are not substitutes for coronary angiography (CO2 only below diaphragm).

## Other drug facts that appear on the exam
- Statin loading before PCI (ARMYDA-ACS, NAPLES II) ↓periprocedural MI; high-intensity after ACS.
- PCSK9 inhibitors/ezetimibe: LDL <55 mg/dL target for very high risk (ESC).
- Colchicine 0.5 mg daily: COLCOT and LoDoCo2 positive, **CLEAR SYNERGY (2024) negative post-MI**; 2b.
- Lipids: high-intensity statin (≥50% LDL reduction); **2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline: LDL <55 mg/dL
  for very-high-risk ASCVD, <70 for other ASCVD**; add ezetimibe, then PCSK9i/inclisiran/bempedoic acid;
  measure Lp(a) once.
- Beta-blocker after MI with preserved EF is contested: REDUCE-AMI and REBOOT neutral, BETAMI-DANBLOCK
  favorable. Transfusion in MI: MINT (2023) restrictive strategy not non-inferior; Hb ≥10 may be reasonable (2b).
- Fibrinolysis adjuncts: clopidogrel 300 mg (≤75 y) or 75 mg (>75 y); enoxaparin preferred
  (ExTRACT-TIMI 25; ≥75 y no bolus, 0.75 mg/kg q12h); half-dose tenecteplase ≥75 y (STREAM); TREAT:
  switching to ticagrelor after lysis (<75 y) non-inferior for bleeding.
- Contrast maximum dose rule of thumb: 5 mL × weight (kg) / creatinine, up to 300 mL.
- Nitrates + PDE5 inhibitors: profound hypotension.
- Beta-blockers: avoid in cocaine-associated ACS acutely (unopposed alpha) – phentolamine,
  benzodiazepines, nitrates.
- Sedation: midazolam 0.5–2 mg + fentanyl 25–50 µg; fentanyl delays P2Y12 absorption.
- Vasopressin and terlipressin not for coronary spasm.
- Warfarin (SCAI 2021): INR ≤1.8 femoral, ≤2.2 radial; no vitamin K. Apixaban/rivaroxaban hold
  24–48 h; dabigatran 48 h (72–96 h if CrCl <50); emergencies go radial. DOACs need no bridging;
  VKA bridging only for mechanical mitral valve or embolism within 3 months (BRIDGE: none for AF).
- DOAC renal clearance: dabigatran ~80%, edoxaban ~50%, rivaroxaban ~35%, apixaban ~27%.
  Edoxaban 30 mg if CrCl 15–50 (avoid if CrCl >95). VKA still required for mechanical valves,
  rheumatic MS with AF (INVICTUS) and triple-positive antiphospholipid syndrome.
- HIT duration: HIT with thrombosis 3 months; isolated HIT 4–6 weeks (up to 3 months); DOACs
  acceptable; warfarin only after platelets ≥150k with ≥5 days overlap.
- Drug-eluting stent drugs: sirolimus/everolimus/zotarolimus (mTOR inhibitors, cytostatic,
  G1 arrest) vs paclitaxel (microtubule stabilizer, cytotoxic, used on DCB because it is
  lipophilic and retained). Biolimus (polymer-free, LEADERS FREE in HBR). Polymer types:
  durable fluoropolymer (Xience), biodegradable PLLA/PLGA (Synergy, Orsiro), polymer-free.
- Brachytherapy (beta, Sr-90) for recurrent ISR; late "edge effect" and late thrombosis with
  short DAPT historically.
