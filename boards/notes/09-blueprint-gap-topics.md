# 09 – Blueprint Gap Topics

Topics named explicitly in the January 2026 blueprint that the other notes cover thinly. These are
finite, factual, and cheap points.

## Preoperative cardiac evaluation and revascularization before noncardiac surgery
- Stepwise approach (2024 ACC/AHA perioperative guideline): emergency surgery → proceed with
  monitoring; active cardiac condition (ACS, decompensated HF, significant arrhythmia, severe
  valve disease) → treat first; estimate risk (RCRI, NSQIP; elevated risk ≥1%); functional
  capacity ≥4 METs (climb 2 flights) → proceed without testing; poor/unknown capacity and
  elevated risk → stress testing only if it will change management. Routine coronary angiography
  or revascularization to "get through" surgery is not recommended.
- **CARP**: prophylactic revascularization before elective vascular surgery gave no survival
  benefit. Revascularize only for standard indications (LM, ACS, refractory symptoms).
- **POISE**: starting high-dose metoprolol on the day of surgery reduced MI but increased death and
  stroke; continue chronic beta-blockers, never start them the day of surgery. Statins: continue;
  start before vascular surgery. Aspirin: POISE-2 no benefit and more bleeding in patients without
  recent stents, but continue aspirin in patients with coronary stents when the surgeon allows.
- **Timing of elective noncardiac surgery after PCI**: balloon angioplasty ≥14 days; BMS ≥30 days;
  DES for chronic coronary syndrome ≥6 months (≥3 months if the surgery is time-sensitive and the
  delay risk exceeds stent-thrombosis risk); DES for ACS ≥12 months (≥6 months if time-sensitive).
  Continue aspirin throughout if at all possible; stop the P2Y12 inhibitor for the minimum time
  (clopidogrel/ticagrelor 5 days, prasugrel 7 days) and resume within 24–48 h; consider a
  cangrelor bridge for very high-risk recent stents. Never interrupt DAPT within 30 days of DES.
- Perioperative MI is usually type 2 (supply–demand) and often silent; troponin surveillance in
  high-risk patients (MINS); manage with aspirin, statin, and angiography for type 1 features.

## Emergency pacing and electrophysiologic complications of STEMI
- Inferior MI heart block: AV nodal level (narrow escape 40–60/min), often transient, responds to
  atropine/aminophylline; pace only if symptomatic or hemodynamically unstable.
- Anterior MI with new bifascicular block, Mobitz II, or alternating BBB: infranodal, wide
  unreliable escape → prophylactic temporary transvenous pacing; high mortality (large infarct).
- Transcutaneous pacing as a bridge; transvenous via right IJ (balloon-tipped) or femoral under
  fluoroscopy; risks: RV perforation (tamponade), VT, dislodgement, infection.
- VF during cath: defibrillate immediately (200 J biphasic); common triggers are RCA ostial
  damping/deep injection, conus branch injection, ionic contrast, hypokalemia, long ischemia.
- Antiarrhythmics in the lab: amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min then 1 mg/min; lidocaine 1–1.5
  mg/kg for VF/VT; procainamide for stable monomorphic VT; magnesium 2 g for torsades; adenosine
  6/12 mg for SVT (contraindicated in severe asthma); accelerated idioventricular rhythm after
  reperfusion is benign, no treatment.

## Right heart catheterization and endomyocardial biopsy
- RHC: access IJ (US-guided) or femoral; measure in end-expiration; confirm wedge (a/v waves,
  sat >90%, mean ≤ PA diastolic); thermodilution unreliable with severe TR or shunt; Fick with
  measured VO2 preferred in low output.
- Endomyocardial biopsy: RV septum via right IJ (Stanford bioptome) or femoral (long sheath);
  ≥4–5 samples; indications: transplant rejection surveillance, fulminant/unexplained new HF
  (giant cell, eosinophilic, lymphocytic myocarditis), suspected infiltrative disease (amyloid,
  sarcoid), cardiotoxicity monitoring; complications: perforation/tamponade 0.5–1%, TR from chordal
  injury, arrhythmia, RBBB (dangerous with pre-existing LBBB → have pacing ready), pneumothorax,
  carotid puncture; sampling error in focal disease.

## Local anesthetics
- Lidocaine 1% = 10 mg/mL; maximum 4.5 mg/kg (≤300 mg) plain, 7 mg/kg (≤500 mg) with epinephrine.
  Onset 1–2 min, duration 30–60 min (2–3 h with epinephrine). Bupivacaine is the most cardiotoxic.
- Toxicity sequence: perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation, seizures, then
  bradycardia, hypotension, wide QRS, VT/asystole. Treat with airway/benzodiazepines, and **20%
  lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg bolus then 0.25 mL/kg/min**; avoid vasopressin, use low-dose
  epinephrine; prolonged CPR/ECMO if needed.
- True allergy is rare (ester agents via PABA; amides rarely; methylparaben preservative);
  alternative: diphenhydramine 1% infiltration.

## Contrast physics and the imaging chain
- Iodine K-edge is 33.2 keV; maximal iodine contrast at tube voltages of ~60–80 kVp; higher kVp
  penetrates more but reduces iodine contrast. Concentration 320–370 mgI/mL; viscosity rises with
  concentration and falls with warming (warm iodixanol to 37 °C). Ionic high-osmolar agents
  (diatrizoate, >1500 mOsm) caused VF, hypotension, and more reactions; non-ionic low-osmolar
  (iohexol, iopamidol, ioversol; 600–800 mOsm) and iso-osmolar iodixanol (290 mOsm, dimer, most
  viscous) are standard.
- Imaging chain: generator → x-ray tube (tungsten anode; small focal spot for fluoro, large for
  cine; ~1% of energy becomes x-rays) → filtration (inherent aluminum plus added copper removes
  low-energy photons) → collimator → patient (source of scatter) → anti-scatter grid → flat-panel
  detector (cesium iodide scintillator on amorphous silicon) → image processing (edge enhancement,
  frame averaging) → display. Automatic exposure/dose-rate control raises kVp and mA as
  attenuation rises (large patients, steep angles, magnification).
- Parameters: kVp (energy/penetration), mA (photon number; dose ∝ mA), pulse width (motion blur if
  long), frame rate (dose ∝ fps), field of view (smaller FOV/magnification ↑ dose), source-to-image
  distance (detector close to patient), table height (patient away from tube).
- Image quality trade-offs: spatial resolution (focal spot, detector pixel, motion), contrast
  (kVp, iodine, scatter), noise/quantum mottle (mA, frame averaging). Digital subtraction
  angiography for peripheral work (mask subtraction; motion artifact; less contrast needed).

## Basic science bullets the blueprint names
- **Clotting cascade**: intrinsic (XII, XI, IX, VIII; aPTT) and extrinsic (tissue factor, VII; PT/
  INR) converge on the common pathway (X, V, II, fibrinogen). UFH potentiates antithrombin against
  IIa and Xa (1:1); LMWH mostly Xa (3:1); fondaparinux Xa only; bivalirudin/argatroban direct IIa;
  warfarin II, VII, IX, X and proteins C/S (early prothrombotic window); dabigatran IIa; rivaroxaban,
  apixaban, edoxaban Xa. Fibrinolytics activate plasminogen → plasmin (alteplase/tenecteplase
  fibrin-specific; streptokinase not, antigenic).
- **Platelet function**: adhesion (vWF–GPIb at high shear, collagen–GPVI), activation (ADP via P2Y12
  and P2Y1, thromboxane A2 via COX-1, thrombin via PAR-1 targeted by vorapaxar), aggregation
  (fibrinogen bridging activated GPIIb/IIIa). Platelet-function testing (VerifyNow PRU >208 = high
  reactivity; LTA; VASP) and CYP2C19 genotyping are not recommended routinely (GRAVITAS, ARCTIC,
  TRIGGER-PCI, TAILOR-PCI); useful for individualized decisions.
- **Lipids**: LDL cleared by hepatic LDL receptors; PCSK9 degrades LDL receptors (inhibitors
  evolocumab/alirocumab; inclisiran siRNA); statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase; ezetimibe blocks
  NPC1L1; bempedoic acid inhibits ATP-citrate lyase (liver-specific, less myalgia); icosapent ethyl
  (REDUCE-IT, ↑AF/bleeding); Lp(a) measure once (genetic, not statin-responsive; pelacarsen trials).
- **Reperfusion injury**: calcium overload, mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening,
  oxidative burst, neutrophil plugging, microvascular obstruction (CMR MVO predicts remodeling);
  post-conditioning, cyclosporine (CIRCUS negative), and most cardioprotection trials failed.
- **Restenosis biology**: PTCA restenosis = elastic recoil + negative remodeling + neointima;
  stent restenosis = neointimal hyperplasia only (smooth-muscle migration/proliferation, peaks 6–12
  months for BMS); DES restenosis is focal, later, with neoatherosclerosis. Diabetes: diffuse
  disease, small vessels, impaired collaterals, endothelial dysfunction, platelet hyperreactivity,
  more restenosis and progression; CABG advantage.
- **Vascular remodeling**: Glagov compensatory enlargement until ~40% plaque burden; positive
  remodeling in ACS/vulnerable plaque, negative in stable/diabetic/elderly lesions.
- **Vasoreactivity**: endothelium-dependent (acetylcholine, flow-mediated; abnormal → constriction)
  vs endothelium-independent (nitroglycerin, adenosine, nitroprusside).

## Coronary ectasia and aneurysm
- Ectasia = diffuse dilation ≥1.5× the reference (Markis classification I–IV by extent); aneurysm =
  focal. Causes: atherosclerosis (most), Kawasaki, connective tissue disease, vasculitis, cocaine,
  post-DES/brachytherapy (mycotic/inflammatory). Associated with abdominal aortic aneurysm (screen).
- Consequences: sluggish flow (TIMI frame count high), thrombus, distal embolization, spasm,
  ACS despite no stenosis. Management: antiplatelet ± anticoagulation for thrombotic events (data
  weak), avoid stenting ectatic segments (sizing, malapposition); covered stents or surgery for
  large/enlarging aneurysms (>2× reference or symptomatic); Kawasaki giant aneurysms (≥8 mm) get
  anticoagulation plus aspirin.

## Cost-effectiveness, quality of life, and epidemiology
- QALY = years of life × utility (0 = death, 1 = perfect health). ICER = Δcost / ΔQALY between
  strategies. US willingness-to-pay thresholds commonly $50,000–$150,000/QALY ("high value" <$50k,
  "intermediate" $50–150k, "low value" >$150k per ACC/AHA value statements). A "dominant" strategy
  is cheaper and more effective; "dominated" is costlier and worse. Cost-effectiveness ≠ cost-saving.
- Angina/HF quality-of-life instruments: Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ; frequency, physical
  limitation, QoL domains), Rose Dyspnea Scale, KCCQ (HF and structural trials), EQ-5D (utilities).
- Epidemiology: incidence (new cases/time) vs prevalence (existing cases at a point; prevalence ≈
  incidence × duration); case-fatality; relative vs attributable risk; number needed to screen;
  lead-time and length-time bias in screening.

## Device surveillance, adverse event reporting, and regulation
- FDA device classes: Class I (low risk, general controls), Class II (510(k) "substantial
  equivalence" to a predicate; most catheters/wires), Class III (life-sustaining/implanted: stents,
  valves, LAAO, MCS → premarket approval (PMA) requiring an investigational device exemption (IDE)
  trial). Humanitarian device exemption for conditions affecting <8,000 US patients/year (no
  effectiveness proof required). Post-approval studies and registries (TVT Registry mandated for
  TAVR/TEER reimbursement).
- Reporting: MedWatch is voluntary for clinicians; under the Safe Medical Devices Act, hospitals must
  report device-related **deaths to FDA and the manufacturer within 10 working days**, and serious
  injuries to the manufacturer (or FDA if unknown). Manufacturers report to FDA within 30 days
  (5 days if remedial action needed). MAUDE is the public database of these reports (passive,
  under-reported, no denominators). Recalls: Class I (serious harm/death), II, III.
- Drug regulation parallels: Phase I–IV; post-marketing (phase IV) surveillance; black-box warnings.

## Consent, documentation, patient safety, professionalism
- Informed consent elements: nature of the procedure, risks (including radiation, contrast, stroke,
  death), benefits, alternatives (including no treatment), the operator's own outcomes when material,
  and the patient's capacity (understand, appreciate, reason, express a choice). Emergency exception;
  surrogate hierarchy; consent for trainee participation; ad hoc PCI should be discussed in advance.
- Procedure documentation (SCAI expert consensus): indication and pre-procedure risk, sedation and
  ASA/Mallampati, access site and technique, catheters and devices (lot numbers), findings with
  lesion characteristics and physiology/imaging, interventions and results (TIMI flow, residual
  stenosis), contrast volume, fluoroscopy time and air kerma/KAP, complications, closure method,
  estimated blood loss, post-procedure plan and antithrombotic regimen. Time-out before the procedure.
- Patient safety: never events (wrong site, retained foreign body, unintended radiation injury),
  sentinel event reporting, root-cause analysis (system focus, not blame), disclosure of errors to
  patients is an ethical duty; impaired or incompetent colleague → report to appropriate authority.
- Health equity items the blueprint flags: documented disparities in door-to-balloon times, use of
  reperfusion and invasive strategies, and referral to cardiac rehab by sex, race, ethnicity, rural
  residence, and insurance; women present later, more often with atypical symptoms, and have more
  bleeding/vascular complications, MINOCA and SCAD.
- Conflicts of interest: Sunshine Act disclosure; industry relationships must not influence device
  selection; research consent must separate the physician-investigator roles.

## Aortic disease and chronic aortic dissection (noncardiac vascular)
- AAA screening: one-time ultrasound for men 65–75 who ever smoked. Repair at ≥5.5 cm (men),
  ≥5.0 cm (women), growth >1 cm/year, or symptoms. EVAR: lower early mortality, equal long-term,
  needs lifelong surveillance for endoleak (types I–V, see note 07) and sac growth.
- Type B dissection: uncomplicated → medical therapy (HR <60, SBP <120, beta-blocker first, then
  vasodilator); complicated (malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain/hypertension, rapid expansion)
  → TEVAR. Chronic type B: TEVAR for aneurysmal degeneration ≥5.5 cm or growth; INSTEAD-XL showed
  late benefit of pre-emptive TEVAR in uncomplicated dissection (aorta-specific mortality). Type A
  is surgical; a STEMI mimic when the dissection involves the RCA ostium (inferior ST elevation with
  chest/back pain, pulse deficit, widened mediastinum) → CT before lytics or anticoagulation.
