# 03 – Complications and Vascular Access

## Coronary perforation (Ellis classification)
| Type | Angiographic finding | Typical cause | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Extraluminal crater, no extravasation | Oversized balloon/stent | Observe; prolonged balloon; often benign |
| II | Pericardial or myocardial blush, no jet | Wire exit, oversizing | Prolonged balloon inflation (5–10 min), echo, watch; consider covered stent if persistent |
| III | Frank extravasation, jet ≥1 mm | Oversizing, atherectomy, IVL in eccentric calcium | Immediate balloon occlusion proximal/at site; covered stent (PK Papyrus 5F-compatible; Graftmaster needs ≥6–7F); pericardiocentesis; reverse anticoagulation; surgery if fails |
| III-CS | Into a cavity (LV, RV, coronary sinus) | Septal collateral wiring | Often benign; coils if large fistula |

- Distal/wire perforations (hydrophilic/CTO wires, wire beyond the target): treat with
  microcatheter-delivered coils, autologous fat, thrombin, gelfoam, or subcutaneous fat/
  microspheres; balloon occlusion proximal while preparing.
- Two-guide ("ping-pong") technique keeps a balloon inflated while delivering the covered
  stent through a second guide, or use a 7–8F guide for both.
- Reverse heparin with protamine only after the wire and balloon are in place and
  definitive treatment is available (protamine causes stent thrombosis if you still need
  the wire/stent to be patent; reverse after covered stent deployed). GPI: stop; platelets for
  abciximab. Bivalirudin: no antidote (short t½).
- Late tamponade after PCI (hours) is classically from a **wire perforation** noticed only
  when hypotension develops; keep a low threshold for bedside echo.
- Rotablation, orbital atherectomy, oversized balloons (>1.2:1), chronic total occlusions,
  women, elderly, and calcified tortuous vessels are risk factors.

## Dissection (NHLBI classification)
| Type | Appearance | Action |
|---|---|---|
| A | Minor radiolucency within lumen, no persistent contrast | Usually leave alone |
| B | Parallel tracts / double lumen, no persistence | Observe if flow normal |
| C | Extraluminal contrast "cap" with persistence after dye clears | Stent |
| D | Spiral dissection | Stent (wire true lumen, IVUS) |
| E | New persistent filling defect | Stent |
| F | Total occlusion, no flow | Stent / rescue |

- Ostial RCA/LM catheter-induced dissection: deep seating of Amplatz/guide, deep dye
  injection; may propagate retrograde into the aorta (aortocoronary dissection): stent the
  ostium immediately to seal the entry, CT aorta; surgery if it extends >40 mm up the aorta.
- Edge dissection on OCT/IVUS: treat if >60° arc, >3 mm long, media involved, or lumen <4 mm².
- Wire in false lumen sign: cannot advance, no pull-back "give", contrast staining, side
  branches not seen.

## No-reflow / slow flow
- Mechanism: distal embolization, microvascular spasm, neutrophil plugging, reperfusion
  injury. Highest risk: SVG, thrombus-laden STEMI, rotational atherectomy, attenuated
  plaque on IVUS, long ischemia time.
- Treatment: exclude epicardial cause (dissection, spasm, residual stenosis) first; then IC
  vasodilators delivered distally (adenosine, nicardipine, verapamil, nitroprusside),
  GPI, IC epinephrine for refractory; support pressure; IABP if hemodynamic collapse.
- Prevention in SVG: embolic protection device (SAFER trial, PercuSurge distal occlusion
  ↓MACE 42%; FilterWire non-inferior). In STEMI: routine thrombus aspiration NOT
  recommended (TASTE, TOTAL: no benefit, ↑stroke); bail-out only. Deferred stenting (DANAMI-3
  DEFER) no benefit.

## Stent thrombosis
- ARC definitions: definite (angiographic/pathologic), probable (unexplained death ≤30 d or
  target-vessel MI without confirmation), possible (unexplained death >30 d).
- Timing: acute <24 h, subacute 24 h–30 d (early = 0–30 d), late 30 d–1 y, very late >1 y.
- Causes: early → underexpansion, edge dissection, residual disease, DAPT failure/non-response,
  bivalirudin without infusion, ACS setting; late/very late → DAPT discontinuation,
  malapposition, uncovered struts, neoatherosclerosis, hypersensitivity (first-gen DES),
  polymer inflammation, stent fracture, bioresorbable scaffolds (Absorb).
- Treatment: emergent PCI with intravascular imaging to find the mechanism; potent P2Y12
  (ticagrelor/prasugrel); GPI reasonable; avoid adding another stent layer if the problem is
  underexpansion (post-dilate).

## In-stent restenosis
- Mehran classification: I focal (≤10 mm: IA gap, IB margin, IC focal body, ID multifocal),
  II diffuse intrastent, III proliferative (extends beyond stent), IV total occlusion.
  Recurrence rises from ~20% (I) to >80% (IV).
- Mechanisms: neointimal hyperplasia (smooth muscle migration, peaks 6–12 months for BMS;
  later for DES), underexpansion (most common correctable cause—image it), stent fracture,
  geographic miss, neoatherosclerosis, drug resistance.
- Treatment ladder: intravascular imaging → high-pressure NC balloon/scoring/cutting →
  DES (different drug reasonable; RIBS IV/V: EES ≥ DCB for BMS-ISR, similar for DES-ISR) or
  paclitaxel DCB (AGENT IDE: DCB > balloon; FDA-approved 2024; preferred when avoiding a
  third layer), IVL for calcified neointima/underexpanded stent, ELCA laser, brachytherapy
  for multiple recurrences, CABG for diffuse multi-layer.

## Device loss and retrieval
- Stent loss: attempt to re-advance the wire through it and re-balloon with a small balloon
  to retrieve or deploy in place; loop snare; twisting two wires; or crush against the
  vessel wall with another stent if in a non-critical location. Peripheral embolization of
  an unexpanded stent is usually tolerated. Surgery if in LM/aorta root causing embolization.
- Wire fracture/retained fragment: snare; if small and in a distal branch, stent over it.
- Rotablator burr entrapment ("Kokesi"): avoid by short runs, no decelerations >5000 rpm,
  pecking; treat by pulling with wire loop and guide extension, second wire/balloon beside
  burr, or surgery.

## Vascular access
### Femoral
- Target the common femoral artery: over the middle of the femoral head, below the inguinal
  ligament (not the crease), above the bifurcation. Use fluoroscopy + ultrasound (FAUST:
  US ↓complications, ↑first-pass). Micropuncture.
- **High stick** (above inguinal ligament/ external iliac): retroperitoneal hemorrhage →
  hypotension, flank/back pain, ipsilateral leg pain (femoral nerve), vagal symptoms;
  diagnose with CT; treat with reversal, transfusion, contralateral balloon tamponade/covered
  stent, surgery rarely. Mortality ~5–10%.
- **Low stick** (SFA/profunda): pseudoaneurysm and AV fistula.
- Pseudoaneurysm: pulsatile mass, bruit; US shows "yin-yang" flow. Treat if >2 cm, painful,
  expanding, or anticoagulated: US-guided thrombin injection (avoid if short/wide neck →
  distal embolization; use compression or covered stent/surgery), ultrasound compression
  (fails with anticoagulation), surgery for infected, rapidly expanding, skin compromise,
  or neuropathy.
- AV fistula: continuous bruit; most close spontaneously; surgery/covered stent if
  high-output HF, limb ischemia/steal, or enlarging.
- Closure devices (Angio-Seal collagen/anchor, Perclose suture, MynxGrip sealant): ↓time to
  hemostasis, not complications; infection (Angio-Seal, immunocompromised), device
  embolization, vessel occlusion in small/diseased arteries (<5 mm, heavily calcified),
  re-access same site after 90 days for Angio-Seal.
- Femoral artery thrombosis/limb ischemia: larger sheaths (MCS), PAD; check pulses;
  embolectomy.
- Cholesterol embolization: livedo reticularis, blue toes, eosinophilia, AKI over weeks,
  ↓complement; supportive; avoid anticoagulation intensification; statins.

### Radial
- Evidence: RIVAL (benefit in STEMI and high-volume radial centers), MATRIX (↓mortality/
  bleeding), SAFARI-STEMI (no difference). Default for PCI in current guidelines (ESC class I).
- Anatomy: radial artery loop (~2%), high bifurcation (~7%), remnant radial (~1%),
  tortuosity; arteria lusoria (aberrant right subclavian, ~1%) makes right radial hard.
- Allen/Barbeau testing does not predict ischemic complications (RIVAL); not required but
  commonly done; Barbeau D (no waveform) is the only abnormal result some avoid.
- Spasm: cocktail (verapamil + nitroglycerin), hydrophilic sheaths, sedation, warm arm,
  smaller sheaths; if a catheter is trapped: more vasodilators, sedation, wait, nerve block,
  never yank.
- Radial artery occlusion (RAO 2–10%): prevention = heparin ≥5000 U (or 50 U/kg),
  patent hemostasis (Barbeau), short compression, smallest sheath, distal (snuffbox) radial
  access; treat early with ulnar compression 1 h. Asymptomatic, but lose the access site.
- Radial perforation: advance the catheter/long sheath over the wire to tamponade and
  finish the case, then compression; forearm hematoma → compress, BP cuff at 15 mmHg below
  SBP; compartment syndrome (rare) → fasciotomy.
- Radial for STEMI, with 6F; two-stent bifurcation and 1.75 mm burr need 7F (or 7F sheathless).
- Hemostasis of ulnar and distal radial; distal radial (snuffbox) ↓RAO (DISCO RADIAL).

### Other
- Brachial: highest complication (median nerve injury, thrombosis); avoid.
- Axillary/subclavian for large-bore MCS; transcaval; carotid for TAVR.
- Venous: femoral vein medial to artery; internal jugular with US; complications: AV fistula,
  air embolism.

## Systemic complications
- **Stroke** (0.2–0.4% diagnostic; higher with LM/aortic instrumentation, TAVR): immediate
  neuro exam, stop heparin decision, CT/CTA, thrombectomy in lab or neuro IR; time is brain.
- **Air embolism**: 100% O2, aspirate through guide, forceful saline injection, support.
- **Contrast reaction**: see pharmacology; hypotension + bradycardia = vagal (atropine), vs
  hypotension + tachycardia/urticaria/bronchospasm = anaphylactoid (epinephrine).
- **Vagal**: atropine, fluids; common with femoral sheath removal.
- **Cardiac tamponade**: hypotension, equalization, loss of y descent, pulsus paradoxus;
  echo; pericardiocentesis (subxiphoid or echo-guided apical/parasternal), reverse
  anticoagulation, autotransfusion, surgery if ongoing.
- **Ventricular arrhythmia**: RCA injection (VF from ostial damping/deep injection, ionic
  contrast), rotablation, hypokalemia; defibrillate, stop injection, correct.
- **Coronary spasm at catheter tip**: pull back, IC nitro.
- **Aortic dissection** (retrograde from RCA ostium): seal with stent, CT, surgery if extends.
- **Radiation skin injury**: see note 07.
- **Ventricular perforation with pigtail/ temporary wire**: tamponade; pull carefully.
- **Endomyocardial biopsy**: RV perforation ~0.5%, TR from chordal injury, heart block.
- **Post-PCI MI** definitions: 4th UDMI type 4a (troponin >5× URL with ischemia evidence),
  SCAI (CK-MB ≥10× or troponin ≥70× URL); type 4b = stent thrombosis; type 4c = restenosis.
