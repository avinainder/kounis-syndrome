# 01 – Coronary Physiology and Intravascular Imaging

## Pressure-derived indices
| Index | Hyperemia? | Ischemic cutoff | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| FFR (Pd/Pa) | Yes | ≤0.80 | Validated vs stress testing; DEFER used 0.75. Gray zone 0.75–0.80 |
| iFR | No (wave-free period) | ≤0.89 | DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART: non-inferior to FFR at 1 y; fewer symptoms/less adenosine |
| RFR, dPR, DFR, Pd/Pa (resting) | No | ≤0.89 (Pd/Pa ≤0.91-0.92) | Equivalent whole-cycle resting indices |
| Post-PCI FFR | Yes | Target ≥0.90 | TARGET-FFR: low post-PCI FFR predicts events |
| CFR | Yes | <2.0 abnormal | Ratio of hyperemic to resting flow; combines epicardial + microvascular |
| IMR | Yes | ≥25 abnormal | IMR = Pd × Tmn(hyperemic). Post-STEMI IMR >40 predicts poor recovery |

**Hyperemic agents.** IV adenosine 140 µg/kg/min (central line preferred, 2–3 min; go to
180–200 if inadequate); IC adenosine bolus LCA 100–200 µg, RCA 50–100 µg (short-lived, AV block
with RCA); regadenoson 0.4 mg IV bolus (no titration, long effect, useful with peripheral IV);
IC nicardipine 200 µg or IC nitroprusside 50–100 µg (hypotension); papaverine (QT prolongation,
torsades – rarely used). Adenosine is blocked by caffeine/theophylline (hold caffeine 12–24 h);
reversal with aminophylline. Avoid adenosine in severe bronchospastic disease; sinus/AV block
usually self-limited (t½ <10 s).

**FFR technique pearls that get tested.**
- Equalize at the guide tip, measure with guide disengaged for ostial lesions and always
  without side-hole catheters (side holes falsely raise Pa and give false-normal FFR).
- Pullback under steady hyperemia to localize the pressure drop; for serial lesions the
  distal lesion's FFR is underestimated until the proximal lesion is treated (re-measure
  after each stent). Apparent FFR of each lesion in tandem is not simply additive.
- Drift: pull the sensor back to the guide tip; acceptable Pd/Pa 1.00 ± 0.02.
- Guide damping/ventricularization (deep-seated or small ostium) lowers Pa and can
  falsely normalize FFR.
- Whip artifact, wire in a false lumen, and pressure recovery are other artifact sources.

**When FFR misleads.**
- Culprit lesion in acute MI (microvascular stunning → falsely high FFR). Non-culprit FFR is
  acceptable in the acute phase but slightly overestimates (FLOWER-MI/FRAME-AMI context).
- Severe LVH, microvascular disease, severe AS (limited hyperemia → FFR falsely high).
- Left main with downstream LAD/LCx disease: measure into the non-diseased branch; a
  stenosed daughter vessel raises the measured LM FFR.
- Bridging collaterals and coronary steal.
- Exercise/very high resting flow (e.g., iFR more affected).

**Evidence.** DEFER (FFR ≥0.75, deferral safe), FAME (FFR-guided vs angio in MVD: fewer
stents, lower MACE), FAME 2 (FFR ≤0.80 PCI + OMT vs OMT: fewer urgent revascularizations, no
death/MI benefit), FAME 3 (FFR-guided PCI failed non-inferiority to CABG in 3VD at 1 y; **5-y death/stroke/MI similar, 16% vs 14%, with more MI and repeat revascularization after PCI**),
DEFINE-FLAIR / iFR-SWEDEHEART (iFR ≈ FFR at 1 y; **5-year DEFINE-FLAIR showed higher all-cause death with iFR, 9.0% vs 6.2%, not seen in iFR-SWEDEHEART, cause unexplained**), FUTURE (trend to harm with FFR in all-comers,
stopped early), FLOWER-MI (FFR-guided non-culprit not better than angio-guided in STEMI),
FIRE (physiology-guided complete revascularization in ≥75 y MI reduced events), FULL REVASC (2024: FFR-guided complete revascularization did not reduce death/MI/unplanned revascularization vs culprit-only), FRAME-AMI (FFR-guided beat angiography-guided), FLAVOUR (FFR-guided non-inferior to IVUS-guided for intermediate lesions with fewer PCIs), FAVOR III Europe (2024: angiography-derived QFR inferior to FFR).

## Anatomic-physiologic correlations (frequently asked)
- Angiographic 50–70% lesions: ~35% are FFR-positive; 70–90%: ~20% are FFR-negative.
- IVUS MLA for a non-LM lesion: <4.0 mm² was the classic cutoff; best FFR correlation is
  ~2.4–3.0 mm² (poor specificity, so physiology is preferred for non-LM).
- IVUS MLA in LM: **<6 mm²** (Western; LITRO study) or **<4.5 mm²** (Asian) → revascularize;
  MLA >6 mm² → defer safely.
- Coronary CT-FFR (FFRct): PLATFORM/ADVANCE reduced unnecessary caths; ≤0.80 positive.

## IVUS vs OCT
| | IVUS | OCT |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Ultrasound 20–60 MHz | Near-infrared light (~1300 nm) |
| Axial resolution | ~100–150 µm | ~10–20 µm |
| Penetration | 4–8 mm (sees whole vessel, EEM) | 1–2 mm (no EEM behind lipid) |
| Blood clearance | Not needed | Needed (contrast 10–14 mL; saline/dextran options) |
| Best for | Vessel size, plaque burden, remodeling, ostial LM, CKD (no contrast), calcium arc/thickness (cannot see behind calcium—shadowing) | Thrombus, dissection, stent apposition/expansion, TCFA, calcium thickness (penetrates calcium), ISR mechanism |
| Weak for | Thin caps, small dissections, thrombus | Ostial LM, large vessels >4.5 mm, poor flow/occlusion, renal failure |

**OCT tissue signatures.** Fibrous = signal-rich homogeneous. Calcium = signal-poor with
**sharp** borders, low attenuation. Lipid/necrotic core = signal-poor with **diffuse** borders,
high attenuation. Red thrombus = high backscatter, high attenuation (shadow). White (platelet)
thrombus = less attenuating. TCFA cap <65 µm. Macrophages = punctate bright spots.
Cholesterol crystals = thin linear bright lines. Neoatherosclerosis in ISR = lipid/calcium
within neointima (late ST substrate).

**IVUS tissue signatures.** Calcium = bright echo with acoustic shadowing (arc, length).
Attenuated plaque (signal drop-out without calcium) = necrotic core, predicts no-reflow.
Echolucent plaque. Positive remodeling (EEM at lesion / reference >1.05) = vulnerable.
NIRS-IVUS: maxLCBI4mm >400 predicts plaque-level and patient-level events (LRP, PROSPECT II).

**Stent optimization targets (imaging-guided PCI).**
- MSA ≥5.5 mm² by IVUS (≥4.5 mm² by OCT) for non-LM; or MSA >80% of distal reference lumen.
- LM (Kang criteria, IVUS): ostial LCx ≥5.0, ostial LAD ≥6.3, polygon of confluence ≥7.2,
  proximal LM ≥8.2 mm² (the "5-6-7-8" rule).
- Malapposition <0.4 mm and <1 mm length is benign acutely; large late-acquired malapposition
  associates with very late ST.
- Edge dissection needing treatment: >60° arc, >3 mm length, involves media, or
  lumen <4 mm² (OCT).
- Plaque burden >50% at stent edge and MSA <5.5 mm² predict edge restenosis/ST.
- Calcium: IVUS arc >270°, length >5 mm; OCT calcium score (arc >180°, thickness >0.5 mm,
  length >5 mm = score 4) predicts under-expansion → atherectomy/IVL before stenting.

**Evidence for imaging guidance.** ULTIMATE (IVUS-guided lower TVF at 1–3 y),
RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI (IVUS/OCT-guided complex PCI reduced TVF), ILUMIEN IV (OCT: larger MSA,
fewer ST, no difference in TVF at 2 y), OCTOBER (OCT-guided bifurcation PCI reduced MACE),
OCTIVUS (OCT non-inferior to IVUS), IVUS-XPL (long lesions). ADAPT-DES: IVUS reduced ST/MI.
2021 ACC/AHA: intravascular imaging 2a; **2025 ACC/AHA ACS and ESC 2024 CCS: Class 1 for LM and complex lesions** (RENOVATE, OCTOBER, IVUS-ACS 2024, 2024 network meta-analysis showing lower cardiac death, MI, ST).

## Coronary anatomy and angiographic views
- LM: AP caudal, LAO caudal ("spider"), RAO caudal for LM–LCx. Ostial LM: AP or shallow LAO
  with slight cranial/caudal, using a shallow cusp injection to avoid pressure damping.
- LAD/diagonals: RAO cranial, AP cranial, LAO cranial (proximal LAD/diagonal bifurcation).
- LCx/OMs: RAO caudal, LAO caudal.
- RCA: LAO (proximal/mid), RAO (mid), AP or LAO cranial (PDA/PL bifurcation), lateral for mid.
- Dominance = which artery gives the PDA (right ~85%, left ~8%, co-dominant ~7%).
- Foreshortening: lesion looks shorter and denser; find orthogonal views before stent sizing.
- TIMI flow 0–3; TIMI myocardial perfusion grade (blush) 0–3; corrected TIMI frame count
  (normal ~21 frames at 30 fps; LAD divided by 1.7).
- Rentrop collateral grade 0–3 (3 = complete epicardial filling); Werner CC0–2 for CTO
  collateral size (CC2 = side-branch-like, ≥0.4 mm).

## Anomalous coronaries (case-selection favorites)
- Anomalous LM/LAD from right sinus with **interarterial** course (between aorta and PA):
  associated with sudden death, especially <35 y with symptoms/ischemia → surgical unroofing.
  Intramural segment on CT is the culprit. Anomalous RCA from left sinus with interarterial
  course: surgery if symptoms/ischemia; otherwise less clear.
- Retroaortic LCx from right sinus: benign, most common anomaly ("dot" sign behind aorta).
- ALCAPA (Bland-White-Garland): steal, surgical reimplantation.
- Coronary fistula: close if symptomatic/large shunt/ aneurysmal; coil/plug.
- Myocardial bridging: systolic compression, "milking"; beta-blockers; avoid nitrates and
  stents (fracture, perforation); surgery/unroofing if refractory.
