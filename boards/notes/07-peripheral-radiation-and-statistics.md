# 07 – Peripheral/Carotid/PE, Radiation Safety, Statistics, and Professionalism

## Peripheral arterial disease
- ABI: <0.9 abnormal (0.7–0.9 mild, 0.4–0.7 moderate, <0.4 severe); >1.4 non-compressible →
  toe-brachial index (<0.7 abnormal). Claudication: supervised exercise (class 1), cilostazol
  (contraindicated in HF), statins, antiplatelet; rivaroxaban 2.5 BID + aspirin after
  revascularization (VOYAGER PAD ↓MALE) or in stable PAD (COMPASS).
- Revascularize claudication only if lifestyle-limiting after exercise/OMT; chronic
  limb-threatening ischemia (rest pain, tissue loss, WIfI staging): revascularize promptly.
  BEST-CLI: surgical bypass with a good single-segment GSV beat endovascular (MALE/death
  42.6% vs 57.4%); without good vein, equal. BASIL-2 (2023): for infrapopliteal disease, vein-bypass-first had MORE amputation/death than
  endovascular-first. STRIDE (2025): semaglutide increased walking distance in PAD with T2DM.
  Acute limb ischemia = symptoms <2 weeks; Rutherford I viable (arterial and venous Doppler audible),
  IIa marginally threatened (minimal sensory loss, arterial often inaudible), IIb immediately
  threatened (rest pain, motor deficit; emergent revascularization ≤6 h), III irreversible (both
  signals inaudible; primary amputation). Immediate IV heparin is Class 1. Angiosome-directed revascularization.
- Aortoiliac: stents (covered for TASC C/D, COBEST); iliac stenting excellent patency.
  Femoropopliteal: DCB (paclitaxel; the 2018 mortality signal was later refuted by
  FDA/SWEDEPAD/large data), DES (Eluvia, Zilver PTX), nitinol self-expanding stents (avoid
  the popliteal flexion zone unless Supera interwoven), atherectomy (no RCT advantage; embolic
  protection), covered stents (Viabahn for long occlusions). Infrapopliteal: balloon, DCB
  mixed, DES (Esprit BTK: LIFE-BTK positive), avoid bare stents.
- Complications: distal embolization, perforation, dissection (flow-limiting → stent),
  access; contrast; cholesterol emboli.
- Renal physiology: unilateral RAS is renin-dependent (ACEi lowers BP); bilateral RAS or solitary
  kidney is volume-dependent and **ACEi raises creatinine (AKI)** with little BP effect. Significance:
  systolic gradient ≥20, mean ≥10 mmHg, Pd/Pa ≤0.90 or renal FFR ≤0.80.
- Renal artery stenosis: atherosclerotic — stenting NOT beneficial vs OMT (ASTRAL, CORAL);
  consider for flash pulmonary edema, refractory HTN with bilateral stenosis, progressive CKD
  with bilateral/solitary kidney stenosis. FMD (young women, "string of beads", medial
  fibroplasia): PTA alone (no stent), cures HTN in many.
- Mesenteric ischemia: stent celiac/SMA; median arcuate ligament syndrome → surgery.
- Subclavian stenosis: stent for symptomatic steal, arm claudication, before/after LIMA graft
  ("coronary-subclavian steal"); vertebral protection.
- Aortic aneurysm/EVAR: repair AAA ≥5.5 cm (men), ≥5.0 (women), or growth >1 cm/y.
  Endoleak types: I attachment site (treat), II retrograde branch flow (most common: lumbar/IMA;
  treat if sac grows), III component separation/fabric tear (treat), IV graft porosity (self-
  limited), V endotension. TEVAR for complicated type B dissection (INSTEAD-XL long-term).

## Carotid disease
- Symptomatic ≥50–70% → revascularize within 2 weeks; asymptomatic 70–99% selected
  (life expectancy >5 y, low periprocedural risk). **CREST-2 (NEJM 2025), asymptomatic ≥70%: CAS +
  intensive medical management reduced periprocedural stroke/death plus 4-y ipsilateral stroke (2.8% vs
  6.0%, NNT ~31); the CEA arm was not significant (3.7% vs 5.3%).** ACST-2: CAS ≈ CEA. **CMS NCD (Oct
  2023): CAS covered for symptomatic ≥50% and asymptomatic ≥70% regardless of surgical risk**, with
  shared decision-making and neurologic assessment.
- CEA vs CAS: CREST (4-y composite similar 7.2% vs 6.8%; periprocedural stroke ↑ with CAS,
  MI ↑ with CEA; age >70 favors CEA; <70 similar), ACT I (asymptomatic, non-inferior),
  SPACE/EVA-3S/ICSS (CAS worse in symptomatic). CAS favored when: high surgical risk
  (contralateral occlusion, prior neck surgery/radiation, tracheostomy, high lesion above C2,
  restenosis after CEA, recurrent laryngeal palsy). TCAR (flow reversal, transcarotid) has
  lower stroke than transfemoral CAS in registries.
- Technique: embolic protection mandatory (distal filter or proximal flow reversal — Mo.Ma);
  predilate small, self-expanding stent (open vs closed cell; closed/mesh for symptomatic),
  post-dilate minimally; atropine/pacing readiness for carotid sinus bradycardia/hypotension
  (can persist 24–48 h, treat with fluids/pressors, hold antihypertensives); DAPT ≥4 weeks.
  Contraindications: type III arch/severe tortuosity, heavy circumferential calcium, thrombus,
  no protection possible, occlusion.
- Complications: stroke (embolic; ↑age, symptomatic, long lesions), hyperperfusion syndrome
  (headache, seizure, ICH after severe stenosis; control BP), bradycardia/hypotension, access.
  Stent fracture; restenosis (radiation, women).

## Venous and pulmonary embolism
- PE risk: massive/high-risk (SBP <90 or shock) → systemic lysis (alteplase 100 mg over 2 h;
  contraindication → catheter-directed therapy or surgical embolectomy, ECMO bridge);
  intermediate-high (RV dysfunction + troponin) → anticoagulation, rescue lysis if
  deterioration (PEITHO: tenecteplase ↓hemodynamic collapse, ↑major and intracranial bleeding,
  no mortality benefit); half-dose lysis (MOPETT) controversial. Catheter-directed:
  ultrasound-assisted CDT (EkoSonic; ULTIMA, SEATTLE II, OPTALYSE), mechanical thrombectomy
  (FlowTriever: FLARE, FLAME registry; Indigo/Penumbra: EXTRACT-PE; PEERLESS: FlowTriever vs
  CDT fewer decompensations). HI-PEITHO/PEERLESS II ongoing. PERT teams. IVC filter only if
  anticoagulation contraindicated; retrieve early (PREPIC 2: no benefit routine filter).
- Iliofemoral DVT: CDT/thrombectomy for limb-threatening (phlegmasia) or selected young
  patients (ATTRACT: no ↓post-thrombotic syndrome overall; benefit in iliofemoral subgroup for
  severity); May-Thurner: iliac vein stent. Chronic venous: stenting.

## Radiation safety
**Units.** Absorbed dose Gy; equivalent dose Sv (=Gy for X-rays); effective dose mSv
(tissue-weighted). Machine metrics: **reference-point air kerma (Ka,r, Gy)** at the
interventional reference point 15 cm from isocenter toward the tube — best surrogate for
peak skin dose (deterministic risk); **kerma-area product (KAP/DAP, Gy·cm²)** — total energy,
best for stochastic risk; **fluoroscopy time** — poorest metric (ignores cine and settings).
Peak skin dose is estimated (Ka,r overestimates PSD when views are varied).

**Effects.** Deterministic (threshold, severity ∝ dose): skin — transient erythema 2 Gy
(hours–days), main erythema/temporary epilation 3–6 Gy (2–8 weeks), permanent epilation 7 Gy,
dry desquamation ~10–14 Gy, moist desquamation ~15–18 Gy, dermal necrosis/ulcer >18–24 Gy
(months–years, delayed); cataract (threshold lowered to ~0.5 Gy; posterior subcapsular).
Stochastic (no threshold, probability ∝ dose): cancer (~5%/Sv lifetime), heritable effects.
Substantial radiation dose level (SIR/NCRP 168): Ka,r **5 Gy**, KAP 500 Gy·cm², peak skin dose
3 Gy, fluoro time 60 min → document, counsel, follow up skin at 2–4 weeks; Ka,r >15 Gy is a
sentinel event (Joint Commission). Typical PCI 1–3 Gy Ka,r; diagnostic 0.5–1 Gy; CTO higher.

**Operator limits.** US NRC: whole body 50 mSv/y (practical goal <5), **lens 150 mSv/y**, skin/
extremities 500 mSv/y; ICRP: 20 mSv/y effective averaged over 5 y (max 50 in one year) and **lens
20 mSv/y** (know both numbers); lifetime 10 mSv × age (NCRP); **pregnant worker fetus 5 mSv** total, 0.5 mSv/month; public 1 mSv/y.
Two badges: collar outside the apron (lens/thyroid estimate) and under the apron at waist;
effective dose = 0.5 × under + 0.025 × collar.

**Dose reduction rules (patient and operator).**
- Distance: inverse square law (double distance = ¼ dose); step back during cine and DSA.
- Time: fewer/shorter cine runs; fluoro save/last-image hold; pulsed fluoro 7.5 fps (vs 15/30).
- Shielding: 0.5 mm lead equivalent apron blocks ~95–98%; thyroid collar; leaded glasses
  (0.75 mm, side shields; cataract); ceiling shield; table skirts; disposable pads; radial
  access at the arm exposes the operator more unless shielded.
- Geometry: keep the image receptor **close** to the patient and the table **high** (tube far
  from skin); avoid steep angulation (LAO cranial/caudal >30° roughly doubles dose and puts the
  tube on the operator's side in LAO); vary angles to spread skin dose; collimate tightly;
  use the lowest magnification (each mag step ≈ +50–100% skin dose entrance); avoid
  "digital zoom" misconceptions; remove the patient's arms from the field; large patients
  → automatic brightness control raises dose exponentially.
- Scatter: patient is the source; highest on the tube (entrance) side; below-table tube
  means most scatter is low → table skirts matter; operator dose ~1000× lower than skin dose.
- Fluoroscopy dose modes (low/normal/high), frame rate, copper filtration, larger field of
  view. Store fluoro instead of cine where image quality allows.
- Pregnancy in patient: not a contraindication when indicated; shield abdomen, minimize;
  fetal dose <50 mGy negligible risk; deterministic fetal effects >100–150 mGy.

## Biostatistics and trial design
- Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN); specificity = TN/(TN+FP); PPV/NPV depend on prevalence (PPV ↑
  with prevalence). LR+ = sens/(1−spec); LR− = (1−sens)/spec. Post-test odds = pre-test odds ×
  LR. ROC AUC 0.5 = useless.
- ARR = control event rate − treatment event rate; RRR = ARR/CER; **NNT = 1/ARR** (NNH for
  harms); RR, OR (≈RR when outcome rare), HR (instantaneous, time-to-event; Cox); 95% CI
  excluding 1 (ratios) or 0 (differences) ↔ p <0.05.
- Type I error (α, false positive), type II (β, false negative), power = 1 − β (usually 80–90%);
  sample size ↑ with smaller effect, lower α, higher power, more variance. Multiple
  comparisons inflate type I error (Bonferroni; hierarchical testing).
- Non-inferiority: pre-specified margin δ; the upper CI bound of the difference must lie below
  δ; per-protocol AND ITT analyses both required (ITT biases toward null → toward
  non-inferiority); superiority can be tested after non-inferiority is met (closed testing).
- Intention-to-treat (preserves randomization, real-world), per-protocol, as-treated.
  Composite endpoints: driven by the softest component (revascularization). Surrogate
  endpoints. Bayesian analysis (posterior probability; used in device trials). Adaptive designs.
- Win ratio: hierarchical composite ranking of patient pairs prioritizing death (used in
  CHIP-BCIS3). Verification bias: short follow-up misclassifies false negatives as true negatives
  and inflates apparent NPV.
- Bias: selection (fix: randomization), performance/detection (blinding, sham — ORBITA), lead-
  time/length-time (screening), recall (case-control), confounding (randomization, multivariable
  adjustment, propensity scores — cannot fix unmeasured confounders), immortal time bias.
  Regression to the mean. Hawthorne effect.
- Tests: t-test (2 means), ANOVA (>2 means), chi-square/Fisher (proportions; Fisher for small
  cells), Mann-Whitney/Wilcoxon (non-parametric), Kaplan-Meier + log-rank (survival), Cox
  (multivariable time-to-event), logistic regression (binary outcome), kappa (agreement
  between raters), Bland-Altman (agreement between measurement methods), correlation r
  (Pearson/Spearman), McNemar (paired proportions).
- Levels of evidence: A (multiple RCTs/meta-analyses), B-R (single RCT), B-NR, C-LD, C-EO.
  Class 1 (benefit >>> risk), 2a (>>), 2b (≥), 3 no benefit / 3 harm.
- Study designs: RCT > cohort > case-control > case series. Registry (NCDR CathPCI) risk models
  for mortality, AKI, bleeding; public reporting and risk aversion.

## Quality, appropriateness, professionalism
- AUC (2017 stable, 2016 ACS): "appropriate / may be appropriate / rarely appropriate";
  rarely appropriate = asymptomatic single-vessel without ischemia, PCI of ≤50% lesions
  without physiology; requirements for antianginal trial in stable CAD.
- Quality metrics: door-to-balloon, radial use, contrast volume, AKI, bleeding, unplanned
  CABG, mortality (risk-adjusted), same-day discharge (safe for uncomplicated elective PCI),
  PCI without on-site surgery (acceptable with protocols; CPORT-E, MASS COMM), operator
  volume ≥50 PCIs/y (institution ≥200).
- Informed consent: capacity, disclosure of risks/alternatives, surrogate decision-makers,
  emergency exception. Refusing care; DNR in the lab (discuss suspending vs maintaining).
- Conflicts of interest (Sunshine Act), industry relations, disclosure of complications/errors
  (duty to disclose), impaired colleague (report), peer review protections, sham controls
  ethics (ORBITA), research consent, HIPAA, patient portal results.
- Radiation and reproductive counseling of staff; pregnant operators may work with badges.
