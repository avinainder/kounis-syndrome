# 05 – Structural Heart Intervention

## TAVR
**Indications (2020 ACC/AHA VHD).** Severe symptomatic AS (or asymptomatic with EF <50%,
very severe AS, abnormal exercise test, rapid progression, or other cardiac surgery).
Choice: age >80 or life expectancy <10 y → TAVR; <65 or life expectancy >20 y → SAVR
(durability); 65–80 shared decision. TAVR favored when: high surgical risk, porcelain aorta,
hostile chest, frailty. SAVR favored when: bicuspid with aortopathy needing repair, low
coronary height, unfavorable annulus, concomitant CABG/mitral surgery, endocarditis.
Pivotal trials: PARTNER 1B (inoperable vs medical: ↓death 20% absolute), 1A (high risk vs SAVR
non-inferior), CoreValve US Pivotal (high risk: TAVR superior mortality), PARTNER 2A/SAPIEN 3
and SURTAVI (intermediate risk), PARTNER 3 and Evolut Low Risk (low risk; PARTNER 3 1-y
death/stroke/rehosp 8.5% vs 15.1%; 5-y results converged for death). Evolut Low Risk 4-y:
TAVR ↓death/disabling stroke. NOTION (all-comers), UK TAVI.

**Planning (gated CTA).** Annulus area/perimeter (systolic phase), LVOT calcium, sinus of
Valsalva diameters, sinotubular junction, coronary heights (LM usually higher than RCA;
**<10–12 mm** plus narrow sinuses → coronary occlusion risk), leaflet length, aortic angulation
(>48–70° horizontal aorta favors balloon-expandable), iliofemoral size (≥5.5 mm for 14F
eSheath; calcification, tortuosity), membranous septum length (short → pacemaker).
Sizing: balloon-expandable oversize area 5–15%; self-expanding oversize perimeter 10–25%.
Bicuspid: Sievers types (0 no raphe, 1 one raphe most common, 2 two raphes); higher
paravalvular leak, annular rupture, pacemaker, aortopathy; use supra-annular/inter-commissural
sizing; not in pivotal RCTs.

**Devices.** Balloon-expandable (SAPIEN 3/Ultra; intra-annular, cobalt-chrome, rapid pacing
required, lower pacemaker rate ~5–8%, higher annular rupture risk if LVOT calcium and
oversizing) vs self-expanding (Evolut PRO/FX supra-annular nitinol: better hemodynamics in small
annuli, recapturable, more pacemakers ~15%, more PVL historically; ACURATE neo2 and Navitor
alternatives). SMART trial: self-expanding better hemodynamics/valve dysfunction in small annuli.

**Complications and management.**
| Complication | Predictors | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction: new LBBB (10–30%), high-grade AVB needing PPM (~5–15%) | Pre-existing **RBBB** (strongest), self-expanding valve, deep implant, short membranous septum, LVOT calcium, PR/QRS prolongation post-implant | Temporary pacer; monitoring ≥24 h with new LBBB; ambulatory monitoring; PPM if HAVB, or new LBBB with QRS >150/PR >240 not resolving; implant depth <3–5 mm reduces risk (cusp-overlap technique) |
| Paravalvular leak | Undersizing, eccentric calcium, bicuspid, malposition | Post-dilation (self-expanding), valve-in-valve, plug closure; moderate+ PVL ↑mortality |
| Stroke (2–4% at 30 d) | Bicuspid, AF, prior stroke, valve-in-valve, more manipulation | Cerebral embolic protection (Sentinel): PROTECTED TAVR no reduction in overall stroke, ↓disabling stroke secondary endpoint; BHF PROTECT-TAVI negative; class 2b |
| Coronary obstruction (<1%; mortality ~40%) | Low coronary height, narrow SOV, bulky leaflets, **valve-in-valve** (stented bioprosthesis with externally mounted leaflets, VTC <4 mm) | Protect with wire/undeployed stent ("chimney" snorkel), BASILICA leaflet laceration, ShortCut device; emergent PCI |
| Annular/root rupture (<1%) | Balloon-expandable, LVOT calcium, >20% oversizing, aggressive post-dilation | Reverse, pericardiocentesis, surgery |
| Vascular (major 2–5%) | Sheath-to-femoral artery ratio >1.05, calcification, female | Covered stent from contralateral access, balloon tamponade, surgery |
| Valve embolization/malposition | Undersizing, pacing failure | Second valve, snare into descending aorta |
| Leaflet thrombosis (HALT ~10–15% subclinical) | Valve-in-valve, no anticoagulation, low flow | Anticoagulate if ↑gradient/symptoms; routine OAC not indicated (GALILEO: rivaroxaban ↑death/bleeding; ATLANTIS) |
| Hemolysis, endocarditis (~1%/y; Enterococcus, Staph), tamponade (temporary wire/pigtail), AKI | | |

**Antithrombotic after TAVR.** Aspirin monotherapy (POPular TAVI cohort A: aspirin alone <
aspirin + clopidogrel for bleeding, no ischemic penalty); if OAC indicated, OAC alone (cohort B).
Valve-in-valve and prior TAVR: BASILICA; TAV-in-TAV planning (commissural alignment,
neoskirt height).

**Post-TAVR PCI:** coronary access harder with supra-annular valves; commissural alignment
during implant; guide catheters via valve cells.

## Balloon aortic valvuloplasty
Bridge/palliation only (restenosis ~50% at 6–12 months); retrograde femoral; rapid pacing;
sizing 1:1 to annulus. Complications: stroke, severe AI, annular rupture, vascular. Role:
hemodynamic instability before TAVR, non-cardiac surgery, to assess symptomatic benefit.

## Percutaneous mitral balloon valvuloplasty (PMBV, Inoue)
- Indication: severe rheumatic MS (MVA ≤1.5 cm²) with symptoms or PASP >50 (asymptomatic),
  new AF, and favorable anatomy.
- Wilkins score (leaflet mobility, thickening, calcification, subvalvular thickening; each 1–4):
  **≤8 favorable**; >8 poorer results (but consider in non-surgical candidates); commissural
  calcification is the strongest negative predictor.
- Contraindications: LA thrombus (TEE mandatory; anticoagulate 2–3 months and repeat),
  moderate–severe MR (≥2+), bicommissural calcification, concomitant severe AS/CAD needing
  surgery.
- Technique: transseptal (fossa ovalis, guided by TEE/ICE), Inoue balloon sized by height
  (height cm/10 + 10 = max diameter mm), stepwise inflation checking MR by echo.
- Complications: severe MR (2–10%; anterior leaflet tear → surgery), iatrogenic ASD (usually
  small, closes), tamponade (1%), stroke (1%), death <1%. Restenosis 10–20% at 5 y;
  repeat PMBV possible.
- Pregnancy: safe second trimester with shielding.

## Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER)
- **Primary (degenerative) MR**: EVEREST II (surgery better for MR reduction, TEER safer);
  indicated for prohibitive surgical risk with suitable anatomy. PASCAL (CLASP IID)
  non-inferior to MitraClip.
- **Secondary (functional) MR**: COAPT (US, 614 pts; EF 20–50%, LVESD ≤70 mm, EROA ≥30 mm²,
  GDMT optimized by HF specialist): ↓HF hospitalization (35.8% vs 67.9%) and ↓death (29.1%
  vs 46.1%) at 2 y. MITRA-FR (France, larger LVs, smaller EROA ≥20 mm², less GDMT
  optimization): neutral. Concept: "disproportionate" MR relative to LV size benefits.
  Guideline class 2a for COAPT-like patients; RESHAPE-HF2 (2024) also positive.
- Anatomic criteria (classic EVEREST): central A2/P2 pathology, coaptation length ≥2 mm,
  coaptation depth <11 mm, flail gap <10 mm, flail width <15 mm, MVA >4.0 cm², mean gradient
  <4–5 mmHg, no significant leaflet calcification in the grasp zone, no cleft/perforation.
- Transseptal puncture: **posterior and superior**, height 4–5 cm above the mitral annulus
  (3.5–4 cm for medial/commissural jets); ICE/TEE bicaval + short-axis views.
- Complications: single-leaflet device attachment (1–2%), leaflet tear, iatrogenic mitral
  stenosis (gradient >5 mmHg; avoid adding clips), chordal entanglement, iatrogenic ASD
  (close if R→L shunt, RV failure, or Qp/Qs >1.5), tamponade, stroke.
- Tricuspid TEER: TRILUMINATE (↓TR, ↑QoL; no mortality/HF benefit at 1 y); TriClip.
  Transcatheter TV replacement (EVOQUE, TRISCEND II). Transcatheter MVR (Tendyne; LVOT
  obstruction risk from neo-LVOT <170 mm²).

## PFO and ASD closure
- **PFO**: cryptogenic ischemic stroke age 18–60 with high-risk features (large shunt, atrial
  septal aneurysm), after exclusion of other causes (AF monitoring). RESPECT (long-term),
  CLOSE, REDUCE, DEFENSE-PFO: closure + antiplatelet superior to antiplatelet alone (RRR ~50%),
  with ↑new AF (mostly transient, peri-procedural ~5%). RoPE score (higher = more likely
  PFO-attributable; young, no vascular risk factors, cortical stroke). PASCAL classification
  combines RoPE with anatomy. Not for migraine (PREMIUM, MIST negative), or decompression
  illness routinely. Device: Amplatzer PFO occluder, Gore Cardioform. Post: DAPT 1–6 months then
  aspirin; SBE prophylaxis 6 months.
- **Secundum ASD**: close if RA/RV enlargement, Qp/Qs ≥1.5, or paradoxical embolism, regardless
  of symptoms; defect ≤38 mm with ≥5 mm rims (deficient retro-aortic rim acceptable but ↑erosion).
  Contraindicated: PVR >2/3 SVR or PAP >2/3 systemic without reversibility (test occlusion);
  sinus venosus, primum, coronary sinus ASDs (surgical; sinus venosus covered-stent
  investigational). Balloon sizing (stop-flow diameter); device ~ stop-flow diameter.
  Complications: erosion (0.1–0.3%; deficient aortic rim + oversizing; presents days–years
  with tamponade/aorto-atrial fistula), device embolization (undersizing, deficient rims),
  AF/SVT, heart block (large devices near AV node), thrombus, nickel allergy (rare
  clinical significance), residual shunt. Post: DAPT 1–6 months, aspirin 6 months.
- Older patients: LV diastolic dysfunction may be unmasked (test occlusion with LVEDP; fenestrated
  device).

## Left atrial appendage occlusion
- Indication: non-valvular AF with CHA2DS2-VASc ≥2 (men)/3 (women) and a good reason not to take
  long-term OAC (2023 ACC/AHA class 2a; prior 2b). PROTECT AF / PREVAIL (Watchman vs warfarin:
  non-inferior, ↓hemorrhagic stroke, ↑procedural complications early), PRAGUE-17 (vs DOAC
  non-inferior), CHAMPION-AF/CATALYST ongoing. Watchman FLX (PINNACLE FLX): 0.5% major
  complications; Amulet IDE non-inferior to Watchman with more procedural complications,
  less device-related thrombus at 45 d? (fewer leaks). OPTION: LAAO after AF ablation ↓bleeding.
- Anatomy: TEE/CT at 0/45/90/135°; ostium width and depth; morphologies (chicken wing,
  windsock, cactus, cauliflower); exclude thrombus (contraindication).
- Transseptal: **inferior and posterior** puncture for LAAO (vs superior for TEER).
- Post: Watchman: warfarin (or DOAC) + aspirin 45 days → TEE (peri-device leak <5 mm and no
  DRT) → DAPT to 6 months → aspirin; DAPT-only regimen acceptable if OAC-intolerant.
  Amulet: DAPT. Device-related thrombus 2–4% (↑stroke; treat with OAC). Peri-device leak
  >5 mm → continue OAC. Complications: tamponade (~1%), embolization, air embolism, stroke.

## Alcohol septal ablation (ASA)
- Indication: obstructive HCM with NYHA III–IV (or II with severe gradient) despite medical
  therapy (beta-blocker, verapamil, disopyramide, mavacamten), gradient ≥50 mmHg rest/provoked,
  septal thickness ≥15 mm (ideally 16–18; >30 mm less effective), suitable septal perforator,
  no other surgical indication (e.g., intrinsic MV disease, papillary anomalies favor myectomy).
  Myectomy preferred in young patients and complex anatomy; ASA for comorbidities/elderly.
- Technique: OTW balloon in first (or target) septal perforator, myocardial contrast echo
  to confirm the basal septal target without remote enhancement (papillary muscle, RV free
  wall), 1–3 mL desiccated ethanol slowly, balloon inflated 5–10 min. Temporary pacemaker
  mandatory.
- Complications: complete heart block ~10% needing PPM (higher with pre-existing LBBB;
  ASA causes **RBBB** typically, myectomy causes **LBBB**), VT/VF, large infarct with
  remote territory, coronary dissection, tamponade, death 1%. Gradient reduction continues
  for months (remodeling); ~10–20% need repeat/myectomy.

## Paravalvular leak closure
Hemolysis or HF symptoms; Amplatzer vascular plugs; retrograde aortic, transseptal or
transapical for mitral; risk of leaflet impingement; rock the device before release.

## Coarctation and other adult congenital
- Native or recurrent coarctation with peak-to-peak gradient ≥20 mmHg (or <20 with
  collaterals/hypertension): covered/bare stents in adults; balloon alone in recoarctation;
  aneurysm/dissection risk, especially native and >1.5 balloon/isthmus ratio. Long-term
  hypertension persists in many.
- Pulmonary valvuloplasty: peak gradient ≥40–50 mmHg or symptoms; balloon 1.2–1.4× annulus;
  dysplastic valves (Noonan) respond poorly.
- PDA closure (Amplatzer duct occluder; coil if small), VSD closure (perimembranous → AV
  block risk; muscular; post-MI VSD).
- Pulmonary vein stenosis after ablation: angioplasty/stenting.
- Transcatheter pulmonary valve (Melody, SAPIEN in RVOT conduits; Harmony for native RVOT):
  endocarditis risk with Melody.

## Pericardial procedures
- Pericardiocentesis: subxiphoid (toward left shoulder) or echo-guided apical/parasternal
  (shortest fluid path); confirm with agitated saline; leave drain until <25 mL/day;
  complications: RV puncture, coronary laceration, pneumothorax, liver injury.
- Balloon pericardiotomy for malignant recurrent effusions.
