36Case selection

Spontaneous coronary dissection

  • Conservative management unless ongoing ischemia or instability; vessels heal within ~30 d; PCI has high complication rates.
  • Beta-blocker reduces recurrence; hypertension predicts recurrence, lipids do not; DAPT without a stent may raise MACE. Screen head to pelvis for fibromuscular dysplasia.
37Case selection

Takotsubo and MINOCA

  • Takotsubo: 90% postmenopausal women, two-thirds have a stressor, LVOT obstruction ~20%, LV thrombus up to 10%; classed as injury, not infarction.
  • MINOCA is a working diagnosis: three-vessel intravascular imaging for rupture or erosion, provocative testing for spasm, CFR/IMR for microvascular disease; CMR is abnormal in ~75%.
38Case selection

Mechanical complications of MI

  • VSR: anterior MI ruptures the apical septum, inferior the basal; RA-to-RV oxygen step-up; shock mortality >80%; surgery, device closure for residual or as bridge; Impella risks shunt reversal.
  • Papillary muscle rupture: posteromedial (single PDA supply), giant v waves, soft or absent murmur, emergency surgery. Free-wall rupture: tamponade, PEA, pericardiocentesis only as a bridge.
39Case selection

Chest pain testing pathway

  • Intermediate to high risk, no known CAD: CCTA under 65 (CT-FFR for 40–90% lesions); stress imaging at ≥65 or with known CAD; PET over SPECT.
  • Low risk: defer testing or CAC. Straight to the lab for CCTA showing ≥50% left main or ≥70% three-vessel disease with symptoms, or for newly reduced EF.
40Case selection

Stable CAD revascularization evidence

  • ISCHEMIA: no death/MI benefit, better angina. REVIVED: PCI adds nothing in ischemic cardiomyopathy. STICH: CABG lowers death at 10 y, viability does not predict benefit.
  • Diabetes with multivessel disease: CABG Class 1 (FREEDOM). Left main: PCI 2a at SYNTAX ≤32, CABG preferred above 33. CTO PCI is for symptoms, not survival.
41Case selection

The patient after CABG

  • PCI improves symptoms, not survival; targets are native vessels 62%, grafts 32%. Graft failure: <1 mo thrombosis, 1–12 mo intimal hyperplasia, >1 y vein-graft atherosclerosis.
  • AUC "inappropriate" only for asymptomatic patients with low-risk stress and normal LV, or intermediate stress on no antianginal. Unstable post-CABG PCI is almost never inappropriate.
42Case selection

Fibrinolysis rules

  • Give within 12 h of onset, ideally ≤30 min of arrival; fibrin-specific agents with aspirin, clopidogrel and anticoagulation; no GPI or facilitated PCI.
  • Absolute contraindications: any prior ICH, ischemic stroke <3 mo, intracranial neoplasm or structural lesion, active bleeding, suspected dissection, head trauma <3 mo, intracranial or spinal surgery <2 mo, severe uncontrolled hypertension.
43Technique

Chronic total occlusions

  • J-CTO points: prior failed attempt, blunt stump, calcification, bending >45°, length ≥20 mm. Prevalence 18–26% of angiograms, ~50% post-CABG; expert success ~90%.
  • Hybrid algorithm: antegrade wire escalation, antegrade dissection re-entry, retrograde via septals (dilate ≤1.5 mm if needed) or epicardials (never dilate). Collaterals do not relieve ischemia; symptoms are often dyspnea.
44Technique

Guide support and lesion crossing

  • Passive support: larger or more supportive shape (AL, EBU, 7F). Active: deep intubation, contralateral wall. Add-ons: guide extension, anchor balloon, buddy wire.
  • Cannot cross with a wire: hydrophilic or tapered wire, microcatheter (Corsair, Tornus). Cannot deliver a device: NC or scoring balloon, IVL, atherectomy. A stiff wire can create a pseudolesion that resolves when the wire is withdrawn.
45Technique

Ostial lesions

  • Aorto-ostial: confirm it is not spasm or catheter damping (nitroglycerin, cusp injection), disengage the guide, let 1–2 mm protrude, flare with an NC balloon; Szabo anchor-wire is the alternative.
  • Any non-aorto-ostial lesion is a bifurcation: wire the other branch, expect spasm in it, and treat the side branch only when it is compromised.
46Technique

Stent loss and foreign bodies

  • Wire still through the stent: pass a 1.5 mm balloon, inflate distally, withdraw into the guide. Wire lost: snare. If unretrievable: deploy in place or crush against the wall.
  • Percutaneous retrieval succeeds in 70–90%. Never leave a device free in the left main or aorta; parking it in a safe peripheral site is acceptable.
47Technique

In-stent restenosis

  • BMS restenosis is diffuse neointima; DES restenosis is focal and mechanical (underexpansion, geographic miss, fracture, gap) or neoatherosclerosis. Image every ISR; OCT distinguishes lipid neoatherosclerosis from fibrous hyperplasia.
  • Treat underexpansion with IVL or high-pressure NC; otherwise DES (different drug) or DCB (AGENT, FDA 2024); brachytherapy for recurrent ISR with ≥12 mo DAPT; CABG for recurrent ISR in a large vessel.
48Technique

Thrombus and embolic protection

  • TAPAS looked positive; TASTE and TOTAL were negative and TOTAL showed more stroke: routine aspiration is Class 3, bailout only.
  • Embolic protection in vein grafts when feasible (SAFER); VAMPIRE 3 supports distal protection only for IVUS-attenuated plaque ≥5 mm; no routine use in native vessels.
49Technique

Access landmarks and sizes

  • Femoral stick over the lower half of the femoral head (about 75% from its top), below the inferior epigastric and above the bifurcation; ultrasound-guided micropuncture.
  • Sheath French size is the inner lumen, catheter size the outer diameter. Hydrophilic sheaths cut spasm, longer ones do not. Radial variants (loops, high bifurcation) ≤10%; arteria lusoria means left radial or femoral.
50Pharmacology

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

  • No heparin in any form: no UFH, no LMWH, no flushes, no coated devices. PCI with bivalirudin (Class 1) or argatroban (hepatic clearance, raises INR).
  • Fondaparinux does not cause HIT but is never the sole PCI anticoagulant. UFH re-exposure only when antibodies are gone, typically after 100 d.
51Pharmacology

Hyperemic agents

  • Adenosine IV 140 mcg/kg/min, IC 200 left and 100 right; blunted by caffeine and peripheral IV; avoid in active bronchospasm and high-grade block.
  • Regadenoson 400 mcg single bolus, A2A-selective, less bronchospasm, reversed by aminophylline. Always anticoagulate and give nitroglycerin before pressure-wire measurements.
52Pharmacology

Ticagrelor and prasugrel specifics

  • Ticagrelor: dyspnea and ventricular pauses from adenosine reuptake block; contraindicated after ICH; maintenance 60 mg BID beyond one year (PEGASUS). De-escalating off it needs a load 24 h after the last dose.
  • Prasugrel: contraindicated with prior stroke or TIA; 5 mg for age ≥75 or weight <60 kg; never before anatomy in NSTE-ACS; hold 7 d before surgery (ticagrelor and clopidogrel 5 d).
53Pharmacology

Vasoactives and antiarrhythmics

  • Norepinephrine first in every shock; dopamine causes more arrhythmia and death (SOAP II); epinephrine more lactic acidosis (OptimaCC); milrinone vasodilates and is renally cleared; vasopressin for vasoplegia.
  • VF or pulseless VT: amiodarone 150 mg then 1 mg/min, lidocaine second; magnesium for torsades; class IC drugs are contraindicated in structural heart disease; accelerated idioventricular rhythm after reperfusion needs nothing.
54Pharmacology

Reversal agents and local anesthetic

  • Protamine 1 mg per 100 U of heparin given in the last 2–3 h, max ~50 mg, slowly, and only after the perforation is sealed and equipment is out. Idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet for Xa inhibitors.
  • Lidocaine 1% = 10 mg/mL; max 4.5 mg/kg plain, 7 mg/kg with epinephrine. Toxicity runs perioral numbness, tinnitus, seizure, then bradycardia and arrest: benzodiazepine and 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg.
55Pharmacology

Oral anticoagulants around the lab

  • Warfarin: radial tolerates INR up to 2.2, femoral 1.8; no vitamin K; no bridging except mechanical valve or recent stroke.
  • DOACs: hold 24–48 h (dabigatran 48 h, longer with CrCl <50) for elective femoral work; in an emergency give full parenteral anticoagulation regardless of the last dose.
56Imaging

OCT and IVUS tissue signatures

  • OCT: calcium = signal-poor, sharp borders, low attenuation; lipid = signal-poor, diffuse borders, high attenuation; red thrombus attenuates, white does not; macrophages = bright dots. Resolution 10–20 µm vs IVUS 100–150 µm.
  • OCT needs contrast clearance and cannot see the aorto-ostial left main; IVUS penetrates deeper and oversizes the vessel by ~10%. VH-IVUS and NIRS characterize lipid cores.
57Imaging

Angiographic views and the LV gram

  • LAO cranial: proximal LAD and diagonals, and the RCA crux. RAO cranial: mid and distal LAD. RAO or AP caudal: circumflex and marginals. LAO caudal (spider): left main bifurcation. Lateral: ostial RCA and proximal LAD.
  • RAO 30° ventriculogram shows anterior, apical and inferior walls and grades MR 1–4+; EF = (EDV − ESV)/EDV. Complications include arrhythmia, staining, perforation and LAFB.
58Physiology

Post-PCI physiology and the microvasculature

  • Post-PCI FFR ≥0.90 is the optimization target (TARGET-FFR); low post-PCI FFR predicts events. Pullback separates focal from diffuse disease; a low pullback pressure gradient means diffuse disease and poor PCI yield.
  • Angina without obstruction: CFR <2, IMR >25, acetylcholine testing for spasm or endothelial dysfunction. FFR works in vein grafts and in the non-culprit vessel of ACS.
59Imaging

CT and CMR facts

  • CCTA has high negative predictive value; a CCTA-first strategy reduced death and MI (SCOT-HEART). High-risk plaque on CT: napkin ring, spotty calcium, low attenuation, positive remodeling. Calcium and stents limit it.
  • CMR is the reference for volumes and mass; late gadolinium <50% transmural predicts recovery; T1 mapping and extracellular volume track diffuse fibrosis in AS and HCM; CT is the planning tool for TAVR and TMVR.
60ComplicationsWEAK BAND

No-reflow

  • Settings: primary PCI, rotational atherectomy, vein grafts. Risk: age, diabetes, CKD, large infarct, long ischemia, male sex. It carries a worse prognosis.
  • Treat through a microcatheter at the distal bed: adenosine, nicardipine, verapamil or nitroprusside. Nitroglycerin does not work on the microvasculature. Prevent with embolic protection in grafts and short rota runs.
61ComplicationsWEAK BAND

Femoral access complications

  • Retroperitoneal bleed: high stick above the inferior epigastric; hypotension with flank, back or lower abdominal pain, often hours later; CT, resuscitate, reverse, covered stent or surgery if unstable. Pseudoaneurysm: painful pulsatile mass with bruit, thrombin injection. AV fistula: low stick.
  • Risk factors: female sex, age ≥65, low weight or BMI ≥40, CKD, PAD, AMI presentation, sheath size and dwell time, anticoagulation. Closure devices speed hemostasis but do not lower complication rates.
62ComplicationsWEAK BAND

Radial complications and air embolism

  • Radial occlusion 2–10%, mostly silent: prevent with heparin ≥5000 U, patent hemostasis, smallest sheath; early ulnar compression can recanalize. Forearm hematoma: elevate, blood-pressure cuff below systolic; compartment syndrome is a surgical emergency. Brachial access has femoral-level risk with limb-threatening ischemia.
  • Air embolism: radiolucent columns, ST elevation, block or VF; 100% oxygen, aspiration, support, flush or balloon to disperse; never inject without a pressure tracing.
63ComplicationsWEAK BAND

Cholesterol embolization versus contrast nephropathy

  • Atheroembolism: livedo, blue toes with palpable pulses, eosinophilia, low complement, creatinine rising over weeks after aortic manipulation; supportive care, no anticoagulation benefit, poor prognosis.
  • Contrast nephropathy: creatinine rises at 24–48 h, peaks 3–5 d, recovers by 7–10 d; definition ≥0.5 mg/dL or ≥25% (KDIGO ≥0.3 in 48 h). Bland sediment, no eosinophils.
64Noncoronary

Adult congenital rules

  • ASD: close for RV enlargement or Qp:Qs ≥1.5 with adequate rims; deficient aortic rim and oversizing cause erosion (0.1–0.3%, days to years, tamponade or aorto-atrial fistula). PFO in 20% of adults; closure for cryptogenic stroke under 60.
  • Perimembranous VSD closure risks heart block and AR. Coarctation: intervene for >20 mmHg, or >10 with AR, LV dysfunction or collaterals. Pulmonic stenosis: balloon at ≥36 mmHg with symptoms, >64 without. Balloon-test for coronary compression before RVOT stenting.
65Noncoronary

Pulmonary embolism and venous disease

  • Intermediate-high risk PE: anticoagulate; full-dose lysis cuts decompensation but adds major and intracranial bleeding with no mortality gain (PEITHO), so reserve lysis or catheter therapy for deterioration. High-risk PE: reperfuse now.
  • IVC filter only when anticoagulation is impossible or fails, never prophylactic. Catheter therapy for DVT is for iliofemoral clot and May-Thurner compression. Chronic venous insufficiency: compression first.
66Noncoronary

Peripheral artery disease and endoleaks

  • ABI first: <0.9 is disease, >1.4 is noncompressible so use toe pressures. Claudication: exercise program, cilostazol (not in heart failure), rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID after revascularization (VOYAGER); revascularize for lifestyle-limiting symptoms or CLTI; bypass with good saphenous vein (BEST-CLI).
  • Endoleaks: I attachment site (treat), II branch backflow (watch, embolize if the sac grows), III fabric or junction (treat), IV porosity, V endotension. Screen AAA once in men 65–75 who smoked; repair at ≥5.5 cm (5.0 women).
67Noncoronary

Renal, mesenteric, subclavian and stroke

  • Renal stenting only for flash pulmonary edema, refractory hypertension or declining function (CORAL and ASTRAL showed no routine benefit); never for a kidney under 7 cm or a chronic occlusion. FMD: balloon alone, image head to pelvis. Chronic mesenteric ischemia needs two of three vessels diseased; stent.
  • Subclavian steal: retrograde vertebral flow, arm claudication, stent via brachial for occlusions. Stroke: tPA within 4.5 h, thrombectomy for ICA or M1 within 6 h (to 24 h with imaging selection).
68Noncoronary

HCM, leaks, and the newest devices

  • HCM: graded by peak-to-peak or peak instantaneous gradient, not mean; obstruction at rest ≥30 mmHg; septal reduction for refractory symptoms with >50 mmHg; alcohol ablation causes RBBB, myectomy LBBB; gradient after ablation is triphasic.
  • Paravalvular leak closure is 2a for heart failure or hemolysis in high-risk patients (Amplatzer plugs, transseptal for mitral). TriClip and EVOQUE approved 2024; JenaValve for native AR 2026; valve-in-valve TAVR needs coronary-obstruction planning (VTC <4 mm, BASILICA).
69Basic scienceWEAK BAND

Coagulation targets by drug

  • Intrinsic (XII, XI, IX, VIII; aPTT) and extrinsic (tissue factor, VII; PT/INR) meet at X, V, II, fibrinogen. UFH via antithrombin on IIa and Xa 1:1; LMWH mostly Xa 3:1; fondaparinux Xa only; bivalirudin and argatroban direct IIa.
  • Warfarin blocks II, VII, IX, X plus proteins C and S (early prothrombotic window). Dabigatran IIa; apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban Xa. Fibrinolytics convert plasminogen to plasmin; alteplase and tenecteplase are fibrin-specific, streptokinase is not and is antigenic.
70Basic scienceWEAK BAND

Platelets, plaque and lipids

  • Adhesion: vWF to GPIb at high shear, collagen to GPVI. Activation: ADP via P2Y12 and P2Y1, thromboxane A2 via COX-1, thrombin via PAR-1 (vorapaxar). Aggregation: fibrinogen bridging GPIIb/IIIa. Platelet-function and CYP2C19 testing are not routine.
  • ACS plaque: rupture 70–75%, erosion 25–30% (young women, smokers), calcified nodule rare; TCFA cap <65 µm. Lipids: PCSK9 inhibitors raise LDL-receptor numbers; add ezetimibe or bempedoic acid for <25% extra lowering, PCSK9 inhibitor or inclisiran for >25%; icosapent ethyl (REDUCE-IT) and Lp(a) once.
