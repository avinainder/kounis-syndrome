# Advisor review: IC Boards Review 2026 (v1 PDF, 95 pages)

Reviewer stance: education advisor for a structural heart fellow sitting the ABIM IC exam on October 6, 2026, reading on an iPad. I reviewed the 00 review source in full, sampled the nine topic notes, and examined v1 pages 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 26, 45, 57 and 87 (cover, contents, corrections table, bullet-heavy §1, P2Y12 table/trials, ACS bullets, structural, topic note 01, trial one-liners).

## Bottom line

The content is strong: current through 2026, organized by exam topic, and honest about where old teaching changed. The v1 layout works against it. It reads as a long reference document: no map of the exam, no retrieval practice, weak signaling. It also has three rendering defects that change or hide facts. The defects matter most. After them, the most valuable changes are retrieval prompts, a blueprint-weighted advance organizer, and making the corrections and cutoffs easy to see.

## What works

- **Problem-centered and relevant (Knowles).** Content is organized by the decisions the exam tests (timing, thresholds, device choice, trial results), not by textbook chapter. This fits an experienced adult learner.
- **The "What changed" table is the best learning object in the document.** It contrasts the old answer with the current one, which targets interference from prior learning. That is where boards traps live.
- **The UPDATED convention** flags corrected facts in the running text. The marker exists; v1 just did not make it visible.
- **Bold lead-ins** ("**TRITON-TIMI 38:** …") give each bullet an author-chosen keyword. That is a good base for scanning.
- **The tables in the source** (MCS devices, IVUS vs OCT, ALI Rutherford, P2Y12 agents) are already well chosen for comparison.
- **Topic note 08** (trial one-liners) is a ready-made retrieval and spacing set.

## What hurts learning

### A. Rendering defects that change the facts (fix first)

1. **Single tildes became strikethrough.** GFM reads `~80%, edoxaban ~50%` as deleted text. In v1, six passages render struck out, including "dabigatran ~80%, edoxaban ~50%" in the corrections table and the plaque-erosion, PAD-mortality and coarctation/Turner figures. A struck-out number reads as "wrong". This is the most serious defect.
2. **Raw `**` markup**, e.g. "**Early (<24 h):**" in NSTE-ACS timing. The `<` before a digit breaks bold pairing, so the asterisks print.
3. **Accidental blockquotes.** A bullet beginning `>15 Gy` became an indented quote box. This hit the sentinel-event skin threshold, one of his logged misses.
4. **Line-wrap `<br>` inside table cells.** These came from the PDF extraction and print as ragged one- or two-word lines ("…synthesis of **II, VII, IX, X** and / proteins C/S"). They add visual noise in the densest, highest-yield table (split attention and extraneous load).
5. **Lost nesting.** One-space " - " sub-bullets render at the same level as their parent ("Switching P2Y12 inhibitors" and its five rules look like five unrelated points). Three sentences became `###` headings ("NTG does not treat microvascular obstruction.").
6. **The outline drops spaces at line wraps** ("High-YieldReview"), and the Contents lists the 14-section review as a single line. The largest part of the document has no visible map.

### B. Instructional design

7. **No retrieval practice.** The design assumes reading equals learning. Rereading builds fluency without durable recall. Self-testing (the testing effect) is the most robust study intervention for this kind of factual-procedural content. Two days out, it is also the best use of the time left.
8. **No advance organizer tied to the blueprint.** Nothing tells him that case selection and procedural technique are 40% of the exam. Nothing tells him which section serves which domain, or how big each section is. Without that he cannot allocate effort, which adult learners need to do themselves (self-direction).
9. **Weak signaling.** About 420 numeric cutoffs (≥, ≤, <, >) sit unmarked in running text. They are the facts most often tested, and the hardest to find again on review. UPDATED prints as plain bold text. Bold is overused, so it no longer stands out.
10. **Flat visual hierarchy.** h2 and h3 differ only in size. Sections run on with no opener, so the reader never sees a chunk boundary. Nothing tells him which section he is in when he lands on a page from search or a bookmark (wayfinding on a tablet).
11. **Measure and size.** Body text was 10.6 pt across 7.2 in, about 100 characters per line, above the 65–80 range that supports return sweeps. On an iPad in fit-to-width, 10.6 pt displays at about 8.5 pt physical size. Tables at 9.2 pt display at about 7.4 pt.
12. **Tables.** Heavy full grid rules on every cell. Centered, ragged multi-line cells from the `<br>`s. The "Current answer" column, the one to learn, has the same weight as "Original guide said", the one to unlearn.
13. **No dual coding** where the content is spatial or temporal. DAPT durations, the STEMI reperfusion decision, shunt step-ups, which side of a physiology cutoff is abnormal, and skin-dose thresholds are all relational, yet they appear only as prose.

### C. Source-text issues (not fixed in the build; wording was not to be changed)

- `t≤` appears where `t½` was meant (metformin, eptifibatide, abciximab).
- Merged bullets from extraction: "(not menses) Significant closed-head…", "Table up and detector down Avoid steep angles…", "VSMC migration … (fibrous cap) Necrotic core…", "J-CTO … very difficult Higher scores…".
- Lost hyphens: "PCIcapable", "proteoglycanrich", "EPICCAD", "Imagingguided", "COMPAREACUTE".
- Stray "i" at the end of both Colchicine cells in the corrections table.
- In §13, the Qp:Qs, SVR and CPO bullets each absorb unrelated formulas (PVR, PAPi, Gorlin, Hakki), so one label covers several facts.
- His logged miss on donor-vessel FFR (falsely low in a CTO donor) is not covered anywhere in the notes.

These are 10-minute Markdown edits for whoever maintains the notes.

## Trade-offs to know

- **Length went from 95 to 132 pages.** The causes are larger type, a narrower measure, section page breaks, openers, prompts and figures. Words per page fell; findability rose. On an iPad, page count costs little and density costs a lot.
- **Keyword columns are only as good as the author's bold.** Where a source bullet merged several facts, the column label covers only the first (see section C).
- **The blueprint mapping is approximate** and says so on the page. It is a judgment of which section serves which domain.
