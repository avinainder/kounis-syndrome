1Case selection

STEMI clock and when to use lysis

  • FMC-to-device ≤90 min, or ≤120 min with transfer (door-in-door-out goal ≤30 min).
  • If PCI cannot meet that, lyse within 30 min of arrival, then angiography in 2–24 h for everyone (pharmacoinvasive).
  • Rescue PCI now for <50% ST resolution at 60–90 min, shock, HF or instability.
2Case selectionYOUR MISS

Non-culprit lesions and the late presenter

  • Stable STEMI with multivessel disease: complete revascularization, index or staged, is Class 1.
  • Cardiogenic shock: culprit only (CULPRIT-SHOCK: more death/dialysis with immediate multivessel PCI).
  • Occluded infarct artery >24 h in a stable, pain-free patient: no PCI (OAT, Class 3).
3Case selection

Aspiration thrombectomy in STEMI

  • Routine use is Class 3 (TASTE, TOTAL; TOTAL showed more stroke).
  • Bailout only, when large thrombus persists with poor flow after balloon dilation (2b).
4Case selectionYOUR MISS

NSTE-ACS: timing and P2Y12 pretreatment

  • No pretreatment with a potent P2Y12 inhibitor before anatomy is known (ACCOAST, ISAR-REACT 5); prasugrel only once PCI is chosen.
  • Immediate <2 h for instability; early <24 h for GRACE >140, dynamic ST or troponin rise; selective strategy for low risk.
5Technique

Radial access

  • Class 1 in ACS: less death, bleeding and vascular complications; 2a in chronic coronary disease.
  • Radial occlusion prevention: heparin ≥5000 U, patent hemostasis, smallest sheath. Operator dose is higher with radial.
6Pharmacology

DAPT duration after DES

  • Chronic coronary disease ≥6 mo; ACS ≥12 mo; high bleeding risk 1–3 mo (2a), then P2Y12 monotherapy.
  • Stent choice never depends on planned DAPT length; DES over BMS in every subset.
7Pharmacology

Atrial fibrillation plus PCI

  • Default at discharge: DOAC + clopidogrel. Aspirin only 1–4 wk and only for high ischemic risk.
  • OAC alone after 12 mo (AFIRE). No prasugrel or ticagrelor in triple therapy.
8Pharmacology

Cangrelor transitions

  • Clopidogrel 600 mg or prasugrel 60 mg only after the infusion stops (cangrelor blocks their active metabolites).
  • Ticagrelor 180 mg any time: before, during or after.
9Pharmacology

Bivalirudin, HIT, fondaparinux

  • Less bleeding than heparin + GPI; more acute stent thrombosis unless a full-dose infusion runs 2–4 h after PCI (BRIGHT-4, VALIDATE).
  • 2025 ACS guideline: Class 1 STEMI, 2b NSTE-ACS. The drug for HIT (or argatroban).
  • Fondaparinux is never the sole PCI anticoagulant (catheter thrombosis, OASIS-5).
10Pharmacology

Enoxaparin at the time of PCI

  • Last SC dose <8 h: nothing more. 8–12 h: add 0.3 mg/kg IV. >12 h: full dose of any regimen.
  • Never stack UFH on enoxaparin or the reverse (more major bleeding, SYNERGY).
11Pharmacology

GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors

  • Never upstream in STEMI; bailout for thrombus (2a) when heparin was used.
  • Benefit is mostly periprocedural MI; no benefit in vein grafts. Eptifibatide and tirofiban need renal dosing; eptifibatide is out on dialysis.
12Pharmacology

Contrast nephropathy

  • Risk flag eGFR ≤60; incidence 12–16% with eGFR <60 plus one risk factor.
  • Prevention: isotonic saline (LVEDP-guided, POSEIDON) and contrast volume (volume/CrCl >3.7 predicts AKI). NAC and bicarbonate do nothing. IOCM = LOCM.
13Pharmacology

Anaphylactoid contrast reaction

  • Epinephrine is the only first-line drug: IM 0.3–0.5 mg of 1 mg/mL; IV 0.1 mg slowly only for collapse. Never 1 mg IV push.
  • Steroids and antihistamines are adjuncts. Prior reactor: prednisone 50 mg at 13, 7 and 1 h plus diphenhydramine.
14Complications

Periprocedural MI (4th UDMI)

  • Troponin >5× the 99th-percentile URL plus evidence of ischemia (symptoms, ECG, angiographic or imaging).
  • Elevated stable baseline: needs a >20% rise to above 5× URL.
15Complications

Stent thrombosis and restenosis mechanisms

  • Early: underexpansion, edge dissection, geographic miss, DAPT interruption. Very late: late malapposition, neoatherosclerosis, fracture.
  • Minimal stent area is the strongest predictor of failure; acute malapposition alone (15% IVUS, 50% OCT) is not treated.
16Imaging

Intravascular imaging rules

  • Class 1 for complex PCI (2025 ACS, 2024 ESC). IVUS, not OCT, for the ostial left main or eGFR <30.
  • Left main MLA <6 mm² = ischemic (4.5 in Asian cohorts). Post-PCI targets MSA >5.5 IVUS / >4.5 mm² OCT.
  • Stent an edge dissection only if it narrows the lumen, reaches media with arc ≥60°, is ≥3 mm, or impinges the catheter.
17PhysiologyYOUR MISS

FFR, NHPR and their traps

  • FFR ≤0.80; iFR/RFR/DFR ≤0.89; IV adenosine 140 mcg/kg/min for ostial, serial and pullback; IC 200 left, 100 right.
  • STEMI culprit: FFR falsely high. CTO donor vessel: FFR falsely low (rises after the CTO opens).
  • CFR <2 is abnormal but cannot say epicardial vs microvascular; IMR (>25) is the microvascular index.
18Physiology

Visual-functional mismatch

  • Severe-looking lesion with negative FFR: eccentric plaque, small territory, prior MI, collaterals, microvascular disease, or artifact (drift, damping, inadequate hyperemia from caffeine or peripheral IV).
  • Serial lesions: FFRs are not additive; re-measure after each stent.
19Technique

Calcium modification

  • Rotablation: burr/artery 0.5–0.6, 140–150k rpm, 15–20 s runs, no decelerations >5000 rpm, pacer for RCA or dominant LCx.
  • Orbital crown advanced slowly. IVL (4 atm, 10 pulses per cycle) for deep or eccentric calcium. OCT trigger: arc >180°, thickness >0.5 mm, length >5 mm.
20Technique

Bifurcations

  • Provisional first, jailed wire when losing the branch would matter, POT with a balloon sized to the proximal vessel, no routine kissing.
  • Crossover is easiest with TAP. True distal left main with a large diseased side branch (≥10 mm, ≥2.5 mm): DK crush (DKCRUSH-V).
21Technique

Vein-graft PCI

  • Embolic protection when feasible (SAFER). No PCI of an occluded graft in the first month (suture rupture).
  • GPI do not help; no-reflow gets IC adenosine, nicardipine, verapamil or nitroprusside, not nitroglycerin. Embolization risk highest in occluded grafts, lowest in anastomotic and ISR lesions.
22Complications

Coronary perforation

  • First: inflate a balloon at or proximal to the site. Large vessel: covered stent via ping-pong guide. Distal or collateral: fat or coils through a microcatheter (epicardial collaterals may need both sides).
  • Reverse heparin only after wires and balloons are out. Tamponade: pericardiocentesis with autotransfusion. Post-CABG perforation can be catastrophic.
23Complications

Access-site complications

  • Retroperitoneal bleed: hypotension with flank or back pain after a high stick; CT, resuscitate, reverse. Pseudoaneurysm: thrombin injection. AV fistula: low stick.
  • 6 Ps = acute limb ischemia; brachial access carries femoral-level risk with limb-threatening ischemia. Closure devices do not lower complication rates.
24Noncoronary

Cardiogenic shock support

  • Norepinephrine first, dobutamine second (SOAP II: dopamine worse). Definition: RA >10, PCWP >15, CI <1.8 (<2.2 on pressors), CPO <0.6 W.
  • IABP: no mortality benefit. Impella CP cut STEMI-shock mortality (DanGer Shock). Routine VA-ECMO did not (ECLS-SHOCK); ECMO raises afterload and needs venting.
25Noncoronary

Hemodynamic signatures

  • Constriction = LV/RV systolic discordance with respiration, systolic area index >1.1; restriction = concordance, RVSP >55, LVEDP−RVEDP >5.
  • VSR = O2 step-up RA to RV/PA (≥5%); acute MR = giant v waves; tamponade = equalized diastolics, pulsus paradoxus; HOCM = Brockenbrough (post-PVC gradient up, pulse pressure down).
26Noncoronary

Pulmonary hypertension

  • mPAP >20, PCWP ≤15, PVR >2 WU = precapillary.
  • Positive vasoreactivity: mPAP falls ≥10 to <40 mmHg with CO preserved. CTEPH: endarterectomy first, balloon pulmonary angioplasty for selected patients.
27NoncoronaryYOUR MISS

Shunt closure rules

  • Close an ASD with RV enlargement or Qp:Qs ≥1.5. Qp:Qs = (SaO2 − MVO2)/(PVO2 − PaO2); mixed venous = (3 SVC + IVC)/4.
  • Do not close when PVR >2/3 SVR or PAP >2/3 systemic without reversibility. PFO closure for cryptogenic stroke, age <60, high RoPE.
28Noncoronary

Carotid disease

  • Symptomatic ≥50%: revascularize within 2 wk. Asymptomatic ≥60–70% with high-risk features and periprocedural risk <3%.
  • CAS gives more minor stroke, CEA more MI; age >70 favors CEA. Embolic protection is required; statin before CAS; CMS covers standard risk since 2023.
29Noncoronary

Aortic stenosis and TAVR

  • Severe: Vmax ≥4 m/s, mean ≥40, AVA <1.0 cm². Low-flow low-gradient: dobutamine (true severe if AVA stays ≤1.0 as gradient reaches ≥40) or calcium score.
  • TAVR non-inferior or superior to SAVR at every risk level. Pre-existing RBBB = strongest pacemaker predictor. Coronary height <10–12 mm plus bulky leaflets = obstruction risk. BAV is a bridge only.
30Noncoronary

Mitral interventions

  • Balloon valvuloplasty: rheumatic MS, Wilkins ≤8, MVA ≤1.5 cm² (or >1.5 with exercise PA rise), no LA thrombus, MR no more than mild.
  • TEER: primary MR at high surgical risk; secondary MR after GDMT by COAPT criteria (EROA ≥30 mm², LVEF 20–50%, LVESD ≤70 mm). Valve-in-ring does worse than valve-in-valve.
31Noncoronary

Left atrial appendage occlusion

  • Non-inferior to warfarin; 90% of AF thrombi sit in the appendage. Indication: OAC-eligible with a reason to avoid long-term OAC (2a).
  • Post-implant: DAPT for 6 mo now accepted instead of 45 d warfarin. Any peri-device leak, even <5 mm, carries more ischemic events.
32RadiationYOUR MISS

Radiation numbers

  • Skin injury threshold 2 Gy; air kerma 5 Gy = follow-up call; peak skin dose >15 Gy = sentinel event.
  • Operator 50 mSv/yr (lifetime 10 mSv × age), lens 150, skin 500; pregnancy 0.5 mSv/mo, 5 mSv total, waist badge under the apron.
  • Cine is 10–20× fluoro; fluoro time is a poor dose measure. Table up, detector down, collimate, low frame rate, avoid steep angles. Iodine K-edge 33 keV: best contrast at 60–80 kVp.
33Case selection

Noncardiac surgery

  • No revascularization just to get through surgery (CARP). Continue chronic beta-blockers, never start them the day of surgery (POISE). Keep aspirin with stents.
  • Delay elective surgery ≥6 mo after DES for CCD (3 mo if time-sensitive), ≥12 mo after ACS, never <30 d. Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3–4 d. Perioperative MI is usually type 2.
34Statistics

Trial arithmetic

  • Power = 1 − β; α = type I error; NNT = 1/ARR; hazard ratios from Cox for time-to-event; PPV falls as prevalence falls.
  • Non-inferiority needs a pre-specified margin and both ITT and per-protocol; an underpowered trial proves nothing. Propensity scores cannot fix unmeasured confounders.
35Quality & regulation

Registries, AUC, reporting

  • NCDR CathPCI and TVT registries; FFR or NHPR counts as ischemia documentation; outliers are often abstraction errors, audit first.
  • Device-related death: report to FDA and manufacturer within 10 working days (SMDA). Donabedian = structure, process, outcome. Public reporting breeds risk aversion.
